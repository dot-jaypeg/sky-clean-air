What Spray Foam Insulation Is and How It Works

Spray foam is a two part material that expands when applied forming a tight long lasting barrier against air movement. It also delivers strong thermal resistance. That combination is the secret to steadier temperatures and lower energy use.

The two main types

  • Open cell foam
    • Softer and more flexible often used in interior walls and roof decks
    • Provides good insulation and strong sound control
    • Allows a bit of drying if humidity sneaks in
  • Closed cell foam
    • Denser and stronger with a higher R value per inch and a built in vapor retarder
    • Works well in crawl spaces rim joists metal buildings and areas that need added strength and moisture control
We match the foam type to each area so you get the right performance. Some homes need a mix. For instance open cell under the roof deck for comfort and noise control and closed cell in the crawl space to handle moisture.

Spray Foam vs Traditional Insulation

Thinking of fiberglass batts or blown in cellulose. Those products insulate but they do not seal air leaks very well. Air moves through and around them which breaks the comfort and savings you expect. Spray foam does two jobs at once which is insulation and air sealing. Open Cell or Closed Cell Which One Do You Need

Picking the right foam depends on your space and goals.

Open cell foam

  • Great for roof decks interior walls and sound control
  • Good insulation and air sealing in one step
  • Allows some drying potential

Closed cell foam

  • Higher R value per inch
  • Adds strength to framing and sheathing
  • Acts as a vapor retarder
  • Ideal for crawl spaces rim joists and metal buildings
Some projects use both. For instance open cell under the roof deck for comfort and noise control and closed cell at the crawl space to help with moisture and add strength at the base of the wall.

Energy Code and R Values

California Title 24 sets energy efficiency rules. Many San Diego attics aim for R 38 or better. Spray foam can help reach that target with fewer inches than batts. Closed cell foam delivers about R 6 to R 7 per inch. Open cell foam delivers about R 3.5 to R 4 per inch. We build up to the right thickness for your area and project type. Learn more about California Title 24 Building Energy Efficiency Standards and the concept of R value if you want deeper background.
