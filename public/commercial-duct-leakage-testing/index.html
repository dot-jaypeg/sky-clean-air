Why duct leakage matters for San Diego buildings

San Diego’s mild climate still puts a steady load on commercial HVAC. Coastal moisture in La Jolla, dry Santa Ana winds, dusty job sites in Otay Mesa, and warm rooftop conditions in Mission Valley all stress duct systems. Small gaps and loose fittings turn into rising energy use and comfort complaints. You pay to heat or cool air that never reaches the rooms that need it.

If your project needs Title 24 compliance, passing a duct air leakage test is often required before final signoff. Even if it is not required, testing pays off by finding the leaks that drive up energy bills and shorten equipment life. Cleaner airflow helps indoor air quality too. That matters for schools, healthcare clinics, research labs, offices, restaurants, and hotels across San Diego.

Ask yourself this. If 5 to 10 percent of your supply air slips out through seams and access panels, how much is that over a year of San Diego business hours and weekend occupancy?

What is commercial duct leakage testing

Commercial Duct Leakage Testing checks the duct system for air loss under a known pressure. We isolate sections of duct, pressurize them with a test fan, and measure how much airflow it takes to hold the set pressure. That airflow represents leakage. Too much leakage means wasted energy, poor comfort, and a code problem on new or altered systems.

  • New construction in San Diego or surrounding cities
  • Tenant improvements in buildings from Kearny Mesa to UTC
  • Rooftop unit change outs and air handler replacements
  • Major duct alterations or added VAV zones

  • Utility incentive or efficiency upgrades tied to duct sealing

We test supply, return, and outside air sections depending on the plans, specifications, and code path chosen by your engineer or contractor. For related checks see Ductwork Inspection and Commercial Air Duct Testing.

Signs your ducts may be leaking

Not sure if you need a test right now? Here are real world clues we see across San Diego buildings.

  • Some rooms are too warm while others stay cold during the same time of day
  • VAV boxes do not hit target airflow even though the fan is working hard which can point to airflow balancing needs
  • The rooftop unit short cycles and runs at a higher speed than expected
  • You hear whistling or feel air movement around seams, curbs, or access panels
  • Dust streaks at duct joints or insulation seams
  • Higher than expected power bills from SDG&E especially during peak times
  • Persistent hot or cold spots near long duct runs or flex connections

Our process for a smooth pass

You want a pass on the first try. We do too. Here is how we help you get there.

1. Pre test review and scheduling

We look over your mechanical plans and testing section, then set a test window that fits your site work. We confirm access needs, roof access details, parking, and any escort requirements. Have an occupied space with quiet hours in La Jolla or Rancho Bernardo? We schedule around tenants to keep everyone happy.

2. Site readiness checklist

A ready site makes or breaks the day. We share a short checklist before we arrive.

  • The duct sections to be tested are installed, insulated, and sealed per plans
  • Access panels, taps, and end caps are in place
  • Dampers and VAV boxes are installed and wired
  • Ductwork is accessible above ceilings or on the roof
  • Electrical power is available for test gear
  • Fire smoke dampers are in their proper test positions per plan notes
  • Any required penetrations or blank offs are allowed by the GC or mechanical contractor
  • A contact on site can answer access questions

3. On site test setup

We isolate the section, add blank offs where needed, and attach a test fan and gauges. We bring ladders, fall protection for rooftop work, and floor protection for interior pathways. If we spot obvious leaks during setup, we document them and let you know right away.

4. Pressurize and measure

We pressurize to the setpoint called out by the plans or code path. Then we measure airflow needed to hold that pressure. The result is recorded in both airflow and a leakage percentage that ties back to your system size or the test standard used on your project.

5. Find and fix leaks then retest

If the system does not pass, we can help locate leaks fast using smoke, airflow tools, and a trained eye. We often find items that benefit from duct sealing or air duct repair.

  • Loose access doors on air handlers and VAV boxes
  • Flex duct connections without proper clamps or sealant
  • Gaps at rooftop curbs and economizer hoods
  • Missing sealant at transverse joints and taps
  • Damper shafts leaking through bushings
  • Plenum seams that shifted during lift or transport

Your mechanical team can seal while we are on site. We recheck the area and retest so you can move forward.

6. Same day report

You get a clear PDF report the same day. It includes the items below.

  • Project and system details
  • Test method, set pressure, and readings
  • Pass or fail summary with leakage numbers
  • Photos of test setup and any main leak points found
  • Notes for inspector or engineer

Need the report sent to the GC, engineer, or city inspector at the City of San Diego Development Services? We can send it directly so nothing gets lost in email threads.

Common duct issues we find in San Diego

Local climate and building styles across the county create patterns. Here are problems we see often.

Rooftop system wear

UV exposure and heat on flat roofs in Kearny Mesa, Miramar, and Mission Valley can dry out sealant and tape faster than expected. Access panels on packaged units loosen with vibration. Gaps form at curb adapters after equipment swaps. Small gaps add up.

Coastal corrosion

Salt air in La Jolla, Pacific Beach, and Point Loma can corrode hardware sooner than inland sites. That leads to loose fasteners on access doors and hood assemblies. We keep an eye on those spots during setup.

Flex to metal transitions

Busy TI schedules in Scripps Ranch or UTC can push duct connections late in the job. Flex duct clamped onto metal collars without mastic is a common leak source. We test, point it out, and help get it sealed.

VAV box seams and damper shafts

Variable volume systems used in office floors from Downtown to University City include many small boxes. Each one has seams and moving parts that can leak. A few quick fixes on a handful of boxes can turn a fail into a pass. Learn more about variable air volume systems.

Plenum leaks above ceilings

Ceilings hide a lot. Open seams in sheet metal plenums and large taps near air handlers often go unseen until the test. A short burst of smoke finds these quickly. A focused Ductwork Inspection can help pinpoint issues early.

A closer look at how ducts leak and what we fix

Want the short list of common leak points we seal during or before testing?

  • Transverse joints without mastic under the drive and S cleats
  • Taps and takeoffs with light sealant coverage
  • Spiral duct seams skipped during a rushed day
  • VAV box access panels left hand tight instead of fully tight
  • Flex duct stretched tight across long spans that pulled loose
  • Economizer hoods with gaps along the edges
  • Access doors that lost gasket compression over time
  • Rooftop curb adapters that did not sit flat on older curbs

We carry hand tools and test accessories to help your crew move fast on these. If a fix is outside our scope, we note it clearly so your mechanical lead can handle it.
