Air Ducts

Air Duct Repair in San Diego & Orange County

Hot back bedroom, weak vents, bills creeping up? The cause is often a torn or disconnected duct in the attic, and it's usually fixable without replacing everything.

The part of your system you never see

Most homes in San Diego and Orange County have their ductwork up in the attic, in a space nobody goes into unless something's wrong. That's where the trouble starts. On a summer afternoon in Escondido or Lake Forest, an attic gets far hotter than anywhere else in the house. Somebody stepped on a flex duct during a roof job. A roof rat chewed through the insulation. A connection that was taped in 1987 finally let go.

None of that shows up in your living room as "broken duct." It shows up as a bedroom that won't cool down, a vent that barely blows, or an AC that seems to run forever. A lot of homeowners assume the system itself is failing. Often it isn't. The air is just leaking out before it gets where it's going.

Signs the problem is in the ducts

  • One or two rooms are always hotter or colder than the rest
  • Some vents blow strong while others barely move air
  • Whistling, rattling or a flapping sound when the system runs
  • Your energy bill went up and your habits didn't change
  • More dust than usual, especially a fine, gray, attic-smelling kind
  • You had roof work, solar installed or a remodel, and things haven't felt right since
  • You've seen droopy, flattened or torn duct in the attic

That dust point is worth explaining. A leak on the return side of the system doesn't just lose air. It pulls air in from the attic, along with insulation fibers and whatever else is up there, and sends it through the house.

What we fix most often

Disconnected runs

A duct slips off its collar at the air handler or at a ceiling boot, and cooled air starts pouring into the attic instead of your bedroom. We reconnect it, fasten it properly and seal the joint so it stays put.

Crushed or kinked flex duct

Flex duct is soft. Foot traffic, stored boxes and roofers all flatten it. A run that's half-collapsed delivers a fraction of the air it should. We replace the damaged section, reroute where it helps, and add support straps so long runs don't sag.

Leaky seams and joints

Old tape dries out in attic heat and peels away. We seal joints and seams with mastic and HVAC-rated foil tape. For whole-system leak problems, duct sealing is often the better approach, and duct leakage testing will tell you how much you're losing.

Torn or wet insulation

Duct insulation keeps cool air cool on its way through a hot attic. When it's torn, missing or soaked from condensation, you're paying to cool the attic. We rewrap the affected sections and look for why they got wet.

Pest damage

Roof rats are common across San Diego County, and they love chewing flex duct. We replace what's damaged and seal around the penetrations they used. If the ducts need cleaning or treatment afterward, we can do that the same visit.

Rusted metal near the coast

Salt air in Point Loma, La Jolla, Coronado and Dana Point is rough on metal parts over time. We replace corroded sections and seal and insulate the repair.

How a repair visit works

Your tech gets into the attic, crawlspace or closet and traces the runs from the air handler out to each room. We check airflow at the vents, look at every connection we can reach and take photos of what we find. Then we come down and show you, on your phone or ours, exactly what's wrong.

You get a written, upfront price before we touch anything, with the urgent repairs separated from the ones that can wait. We keep common materials on the truck, so a lot of repairs get done on the same visit. Bigger jobs get scheduled for a return trip. When we're done, we check airflow again and walk you through what changed.

Repair or replace?

This is the question everyone asks, and the honest answer is that most duct systems can be repaired. Repair usually makes sense if the damage is limited to a few sections and the rest of the ductwork is in decent shape.

Replacement starts to make more sense when the ducts are brittle all over, when insulation is failing throughout, when the layout was poor from the start, or when you'd be patching so many spots that it adds up to a new system anyway. That's common in some of the original 1950s–70s ductwork we see in Clairemont, Allied Gardens and North Park, and in older tract homes across Irvine and Mission Viejo. If that's where you are, we'll talk through air duct replacement and show you the photos so you can decide for yourself.

Why a quick fix in the attic can matter so much

In the hotter inland areas like El Cajon, Santee, Poway and Escondido, a leaky duct in the attic is working against you all summer. Your AC runs longer to make up for air it's losing, which means higher bills and more wear on equipment. A repair that takes an afternoon can make the whole house feel different, and it takes some strain off your system.

While we're in the attic, we'll also look over the rest of your HVAC setup. If the equipment is around 15 years old or more, we'll let you know how it looks and whether it's worth planning ahead for. No pressure. Plenty of older systems still have good years left.

Air Duct Repair: Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if it's my ducts or my AC?

If the whole house is warm, it's more likely the equipment. If certain rooms are the problem and others are fine, the ducts are a strong suspect. We check both on the visit.

Can you repair ducts the same day?

Often, yes. Reconnections, sealing and replacing a damaged section are usually handled on the spot. Larger jobs or tough attic access may need a second visit.

Is duct tape okay for fixing ducts?

Standard cloth duct tape doesn't hold up well in attic heat. We use mastic and HVAC-rated foil tape, along with proper mechanical fasteners on connections.

Will fixing my ducts lower my energy bill?

If your ducts are leaking conditioned air into the attic, sealing and repairing them means your system doesn't have to work as hard. How much you save depends on how bad the leaks were.

Do I need to replace all my ductwork?

Usually not. Most homes do fine with targeted repairs. We'll show you photos of what we find and give you both options when replacement is worth considering.

Do you repair ducts in commercial buildings?

Yes. We handle commercial duct repair as well. See commercial air duct repair for more.
