What types of duct repairs do you handle

Duct systems are a mix of parts. Repair work usually falls into a few categories.

Sealing leaks and joint gaps

Leaky joints are common at transitions and connections. We reseal where air escapes, especially near plenums, takeoffs, elbows and connectors, and register boots.

Reconnecting loose or disconnected ducts

A duct can slip off a collar or separate at a joint. When that happens, you might be heating or cooling an attic or wall cavity instead of your room.

Replacing damaged duct sections

When a section is crushed, torn, or deteriorated, a replacement is often cleaner than layering patch after patch. If a larger change is needed, Air Duct Replacement in Liberty Station, CA may be the right next step.

Supporting and straightening flexible ducts

Flex duct works fine when it is installed and supported correctly. When it sags, it becomes a speed bump for airflow. We add support and adjust routing where possible.

Repairing or improving duct insulation

Damaged insulation can lead to comfort issues and condensation risk. Restoring insulation on accessible runs helps stabilize delivery air temperature.

What is the difference between duct repair and duct cleaning

They are related, but they are not the same job.

Duct cleaning focuses on removing dust and debris from inside the duct system where accessible. Duct repair focuses on fixing how the duct system is built and connected so air goes where it should.

If your ducts are clean but leaking, you still have comfort and efficiency issues. If your ducts are tight but dirty, you might still have dust and odor concerns. Sky Clean Air is an air duct cleaning service, and we also handle duct repair needs that show up during inspections. Many Liberty Station customers end up doing a mix, depending on what the system tells us once we look.

Can duct leaks affect dust, odors, and indoor comfort

What does a duct inspection look for

A good inspection is part detective work, part basic building science.

We typically look for

  • Disconnected runs or loose collars
  • Tears, kinks, and compression in flex duct
  • Gaps at joints and transitions
  • Poor support and long sagging spans
  • Damaged insulation or exposed metal duct surfaces
  • Register boots that are not sealed to the ceiling or wall
  • Signs of debris entry at openings and gaps

If access is limited, we tell you what we can and cannot see. Some buildings make duct access easy. Others make it feel like the ductwork was installed first and the house was built around it.

What about noisy ducts and whistling vents

Noise is usually air velocity, vibration, or loose parts. The fix depends on the source.

Common noise causes

  • Loose register or grille screws
  • Gaps around the register boot
  • A duct joint that is not secured well
  • Crimped or undersized sections that create a whistle
  • Flex duct rubbing against framing

We track the sound to its likely origin and secure or reseal the components involved. Sometimes a small adjustment makes a big difference. Sometimes the duct run needs support so it stops flapping like a flag in a breeze.
