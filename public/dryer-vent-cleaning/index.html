Dryer Vents

Dryer Vent Cleaning in San Diego & Orange County

Faster drying, a cooler laundry room and a lot less lint sitting in a hot duct. We clean the full vent line from the dryer to the outside cap.

Start with a simple test

Run a normal load of towels. If it takes more than one cycle to dry, or the top of the dryer is too hot to keep your hand on, or the laundry room turns into a sauna, there's a good chance your dryer vent is clogged.

Now go outside and find where the vent comes out of the house, on a wall or up on the roof. With the dryer running, you should feel a strong, steady stream of warm air and see the flap open wide. If it's barely moving, the air is getting stuck somewhere between the dryer and that opening. Usually, what's stopping it is lint.

Why lint in the vent is a safety issue

We don't want to scare anyone, but this part matters. Lint is fine, dry and very flammable. Your dryer's lint screen catches most of it, but some always gets past and settles inside the vent line. As that layer builds up, airflow drops, heat builds, and you end up with a hot duct packed with fuel. Lint buildup is one of the leading causes of dryer fires in homes.

The good news is that it's very preventable. A clear vent lets hot air move out of the house the way it's supposed to. That's the core of what this service is for. Faster drying and lower bills are nice side effects.

A couple of other things that help between cleanings:

  • Clean the lint screen every load, without exception.
  • Don't run the dryer when you're leaving the house or going to bed.
  • If you have a gas dryer and ever smell gas, turn it off and call your gas utility right away. That's not a vent-cleaning problem.

Other signs your vent needs attention

  • Clothes come out damp or wrinkled and hot
  • A burning or scorched smell while the dryer runs
  • Lint collecting around the dryer door or behind the machine
  • The dryer shuts off mid-cycle or shows an airflow or heat error
  • A bird's nest or lint visible at the outside vent cap
  • It's been more than a year since anyone cleaned the line, or you don't know if anyone ever has

What we do on the visit

Your tech starts by looking at the whole setup: the transition hose behind the dryer, the path the vent takes through the wall or attic, and where it ends outside. We check airflow at the exit before we clean, so you'll have something to compare against afterward.

Then we carefully pull the dryer out, disconnect the transition hose and cap the dryer outlet so lint doesn't blow into the room. We feed flexible rods with a brush head through the full length of the vent while a HEPA-filtered vacuum pulls the loosened lint out. On long runs or roof vents, we work from both ends if we need to. Stubborn clumps of wet, packed lint in low spots get broken up and removed.

Next comes the outside. We clear the wall hood or roof cap, free up stuck flaps and dampers, and remove any nests or screens that trap lint. We also clean out the lint trap housing on the dryer itself.

Finally, we reconnect the dryer with a clean, smooth path, clamp it securely, push the machine back without crushing the hose, and test airflow again. Most people are surprised by how much lint comes out. We're happy to show you.

Where San Diego and OC dryer vents go wrong

The setup of your home has a lot to do with how fast the vent clogs.

Roof vents. Plenty of townhomes and condos, from Mission Valley and UTC to Irvine and Aliso Viejo, vent the dryer straight up through the attic to the roof. Lint has to travel uphill, and roof caps with dampers tend to jam. These clog faster than a short wall vent.

Beach air. In Ocean Beach, Pacific Beach, Oceanside, Huntington Beach and Dana Point, salt and fine sand build up on exterior vent hoods. Lint grabs onto that rough surface and the opening closes up faster than you'd expect.

Older homes. Houses from the 1950s–70s in Clairemont, North Park and Point Loma sometimes have vents running through crawlspaces with sags and dips. Moisture collects in the low spots, and wet lint packs down hard.

Remodels and additions. ADUs, garage conversions and laundry rooms that got moved during a remodel often end up with long runs, too many elbows, or plastic accordion hose that kinks behind the machine. If we find a layout problem, we'll tell you. Sometimes rerouting the vent or adding a booster fan is the real fix.

Busy households. Large families, pets with long coats, and short-term rentals with constant laundry turnover all fill a vent faster.

When cleaning isn't enough

If the vent is crushed inside a wall, disconnected in the attic, or made of old foil or plastic, cleaning it won't make it safe. We'll show you what we found and quote a dryer vent repair separately. You decide whether to go ahead. We won't do extra work without your okay.

Dryer Vent Cleaning: Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I have my dryer vent cleaned?

Once a year is a good rule for most homes. If you do a lot of laundry, have pets, run a rental, or have a long or roof-mounted vent, every six months to a year is safer.

How long does a dryer vent cleaning take?

A typical wall-vented home is usually quick, often an hour or less. Long runs, roof vents or hard-to-reach setups take longer.

Can I clean my dryer vent myself?

You can clean the lint screen and the first stretch of hose behind the dryer. The rest of the line, especially through walls, attics or up to the roof, is hard to clean well without the right rods and vacuum, and it's easy to push a clog further in.

Do you clean gas and electric dryers?

Yes. Both need a clear vent to work safely. Gas line work isn't part of a vent cleaning, so if you ever smell gas, turn off the dryer and call your gas utility.

Do you need to go on my roof?

If your dryer vents to the roof, yes, we'll need to clear the roof cap. Your tech will check access when they arrive.

Will cleaning the vent make my dryer work better?

In most cases, yes. When the vent is clogged, your dryer can't get the damp air out, so loads take longer. Clearing it usually brings drying time back to normal. If it doesn't, the problem may be with the dryer itself, and we'll let you know.
