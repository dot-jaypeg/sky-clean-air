How to tell if your dryer vent is clogged

Some signs are loud. Some are sneaky. Common clues in Colina del Sol homes and rentals include longer dry times, extra heat, humidity in the laundry area, weak movement at the outside flap, lint around the dryer, or early shutoff. If you are thinking it still dries, just takes forever, that is often the vent talking.

  • Clothes take longer to dry, especially towels and jeans
  • The dryer feels hotter than usual on the outside
  • The laundry area gets humid or smells a bit musty
  • The outside vent flap barely moves when the dryer runs
  • Lint shows up around the dryer or on the floor behind it
  • The dryer shuts off early or seems to struggle mid cycle

Why dryer vent cleaning matters

Lint is very combustible. A vent line that is packed with lint can create conditions you do not want in a hot appliance pushing air through a restricted tube. For background on why lint buildup is a known concern, see https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clothes_dryer.

Dryer vent cleaning can help reduce overheating from poor exhaust flow, lint accumulation in hidden sections of duct, moisture lingering in the laundry area, and wear and tear from the dryer working harder than it should. No scare tactics here. Just the basics. Dryers move hot, moist air. The vent needs to stay open and clear so that air can leave the building.

Lint screen tips and what you can do at home

Your lint screen is important. It is also only part of the story. A few practical steps can help you spot problems early.

  • Do a quick outside check and see if the vent flap moves when the dryer runs

Dryer vent cleaning targets the vent line, not a full dryer teardown. If your dryer has internal issues, we can still clean the vent and let you know what we observe from the airflow and exhaust behavior.
