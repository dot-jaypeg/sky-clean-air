What Is Blown-In Insulation?

Blown-in insulation is loose-fill material installed with a hose. We use a high-powered machine to deliver the fibers into your attic at the right depth and density. It fills gaps around framing and covers irregular surfaces that batts often miss. That coverage helps stop heat flow and slows air movement through the attic floor.

Common materials

  • Fiberglass blown-in
    • Light, effective, and widely used
    • Good thermal performance
    • Resists mold growth when kept dry
  • Cellulose blown-in
    • Made from treated plant fiber
    • Dense coverage that helps reduce air movement
    • Great for sound control
  • Mineral wool blown-in
    • High temperature tolerance
    • Good acoustic performance

Not sure which material fits your building. We explain options on site and recommend what makes sense for your roof assembly, use type, and budget.

Recommended R-Values For San Diego Attics

R-value measures resistance to heat flow. More R means better insulation. In our area, many commercial attics target R 30 to R 38, and some go to R 49. The right number depends on the following.

  • Roof type and attic height
  • Existing insulation
  • Building use and operating hours
  • Coastal vs inland location
  • Local energy code for your project scope

We measure current levels, check ventilation, and suggest the right finish depth. You will see depth rulers installed so you can verify coverage at a glance. If old material needs to be removed we can include Commercial Attic Insulation Removal.

Our Step-by-Step Installation Process

A good installation is more than blowing fluffy material into a space. It takes planning, prep, and careful work. Here is how Sky Clean Air handles your project.

1 On-site assessment and quote

  • Walkthrough of your building and attic access points
  • Infrared scan or spot checks to find hot spots and missing insulation
  • Review of mechanicals, can lights, and fire clearances

Have a tight timeline or tenants with strict hours. Tell us. We schedule to fit your operations. Ask about pairing this visit with an Commercial Attic Energy Efficiency Inspection.

2 Prep and protection

  • Cover walkways, desks, shelving, and equipment under work zones
  • Protect HVAC equipment and returns
  • Set up containment where needed to keep dust out of public areas
  • Stage the blower and insulation outside to reduce noise inside

3 Attic air sealing

Insulation works much better when air leaks are sealed first. We focus on the big offenders. You can include dedicated Commercial Attic Air Sealing for best results.

  • Top plates and wall to ceiling gaps
  • Plumbing, electrical, and data penetrations
  • Gaps around exhaust fans and chases
  • Openings around duct boots
  • Attic hatches and scuttle covers

Sealing cuts drafts and prevents conditioned air from slipping into the attic. Less air leakage means more comfort for the same energy spend.

4 Ventilation setup

Good attic ventilation reduces moisture issues and keeps roof temps more stable. We install baffles at soffit vents so insulation does not block airflow. We also dam around eaves, access points, and equipment pads. If vents are missing or crushed, we can add or repair vent chutes. We can also plan Commercial Attic Ventilation Upgrades when needed.

5 Safety clearances and dams

We maintain safe gaps around heat sources and code required areas.

  • Furnace flues
  • Non IC rated recessed lights and transformers
  • Electrical panels or junction boxes
  • Access paths and equipment service areas

We build dams so insulation stays put and techs can service equipment later without making a mess. If you want additional verification we can include an Attic Inspection.

6 Blow-in installation

  • Calibrate the machine for the chosen material and target R value
  • Install depth rulers across the attic
  • Blow insulation evenly across all open areas
  • Top up low spots and hard to reach corners
  • Finish with a level, fluffy field that matches the plan

7 Final check and cleanup

  • Inspect with you or your rep
  • Take after photos for your records
  • Clean all work areas and remove debris
  • Review tips to keep the attic in good shape

You get an attic that performs like it should and a workspace that looks ready for business.

Attic Air Sealing Makes Insulation Work Better

Have you ever felt a draft near a ceiling fixture. That is attic air sneaking into your conditioned space. Sealing those leaks cuts the stack effect that pulls air through cracks and gaps. It also limits dust movement into ducts and rooms.

Our team takes the following steps.

  • Uses foam and mastic at penetrations and top plates
  • Seals around duct boots to reduce leakage at registers
  • Adds gasketed covers at attic hatches
  • Recommends Duct Sealing if your system needs it

Air sealing plus insulation gives predictable comfort across suites and zones. It also reduces how often your AC and heat cycle. If ducts need care we can include Ductwork Inspection and Duct Leakage Testing.

Materials We Use And Why

Each building has different needs. Here is a quick guide to help you decide.

Fiberglass blown-in

  • Strong thermal performance for the cost
  • Good for larger, open attics that need higher R values
  • Does not settle much over time

Best for Offices, retail, and schools that want reliable energy savings and wide coverage.

Cellulose blown-in

  • Dense coverage that can help with sound and air movement
  • Often made from recycled content with fire treatment
  • Great for older buildings with many small gaps

Best for Older offices and mixed use buildings with lots of penetrations in the attic floor and a need for better acoustics.

Mineral wool blown-in

  • High temperature tolerance
  • Solid acoustic control

Best for Buildings near freeways or flight paths, or spaces with higher temperature equipment nearby where extra peace of mind helps.

Not sure which route to pick. We bring samples, explain pros and cons, and match the material to your goals and budget.

Ventilation And Moisture Control

San Diego’s marine layer and cool nights can load moisture onto metal ducts. You might see sweating on bare metal or rusty hangers. Proper insulation reduces surface condensation by raising surface temperature. Proper airflow keeps the attic from trapping damp air.

We help you get both right.

  • Keep soffit vents open with installed baffles
  • Maintain clear paths to ridge vents or roof vents
  • Seal bath fan ducts so they vent outside, not into the attic
  • Wrap ducts that need more protection from condensation

Better ventilation plus insulation gives you a dry, stable attic that protects your roof and your HVAC. We can recommend the right Commercial Attic Ventilation Upgrades for your building.

Commercial Blown-In Insulation: Frequently Asked Questions

What is blown-in attic insulation?

Blown-in insulation is loose material that is blown into your attic using special equipment, filling gaps to help regulate your home's temperature.

How does blown-in insulation benefit my home?

It improves energy efficiency, keeps indoor temperatures more consistent, and can lower heating and cooling costs.

Is blown-in attic insulation suitable for commercial buildings?

Yes, blown-in insulation works well in commercial attics, providing effective coverage and improved comfort.

How long does the installation process take?

Most commercial attic installations can be completed in one or two days, depending on the size and accessibility of the space.

Will blown-in insulation make a mess in my building?

Professional installers like Sky Clean Air take steps to protect your property and clean up thoroughly after the job.

Can blown-in attic insulation help with noise reduction?

Yes, it can reduce noise by adding a barrier that dampens sound between your attic and workspaces below.

How do I know if my attic needs more insulation?

Signs include high energy bills, uneven temperatures, or visible gaps and low spots in your existing attic insulation.

Do I need to leave the building during installation?

Usually, you can stay on the premises, as the installation is typically done in the attic space with minimal disruption.
