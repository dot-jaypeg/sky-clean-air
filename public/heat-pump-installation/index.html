Picking the right type of heat pump

Ducted central heat pump

This option uses your existing ductwork or new ducts to serve the whole home. It looks and feels like a standard central HVAC system with supply registers in rooms and a return grille in the hallway.

Best for

  • Single family homes with ducts that are in fair shape
  • Homeowners who want a clean look with no indoor wall units
  • Even comfort across multiple rooms

Ductless mini split

A ductless mini split uses small refrigerant lines to connect an outdoor unit to one or more indoor air handlers. These indoor units can be wall mounted, floor mounted, or recessed ceiling cassettes. Explore ductless system installation options for your layout.

Best for

  • Homes without ducts or with limited attic space
  • Room additions, garages, sunrooms, and ADUs
  • Spots where you want independent control like a home office or primary bedroom

Multi zone heat pump

Run two to eight indoor units off one outdoor unit. Each zone has its own remote or thermostat. This pairs well with HVAC zoning.

Great for

  • Two story homes with different comfort needs upstairs and downstairs
  • Families with different temperature preferences
  • Spaces with mixed use like guest rooms that sit empty most of the year

Inverter technology in plain language

Older systems turn on at full blast, then off, then on again. Inverter heat pumps adjust their speed to match your needs. They run quietly, hold steady temps, and use less energy. Think of it like cruise control on the freeway from La Jolla to Del Mar. Smooth and efficient. Learn more about inverter technology.

Energy efficiency and savings

Heat pumps are known for efficiency, especially in a mild climate. Labels like SEER and HSPF rate cooling and heating performance. Higher ratings point to better efficiency. We help you pick a model that gives strong performance without overpaying for features you do not need. See general guidance from the U.S. Department of Energy.

Tips for real world savings in San Diego

  • Use a smart thermostat to shift cooling to off peak times under SDG&E plans and consider professional thermostat installation
  • Keep filters clean for healthy airflow
  • Seal ducts and clean ducts to avoid leaks and dust
  • Set moderate temperatures. Try 75 to 78 in summer and 68 to 70 in cooler months
  • Use ceiling fans to boost comfort so the system can run at lower speeds

Many homes that replace an older AC and gas furnace with a modern heat pump see lower energy costs across the year. The biggest gains come from proper sizing, good ductwork, and smart controls.

Ductwork matters more than you think

A heat pump can only do its job if air moves freely. Many San Diego homes have older ducts with leaks, dust, or crushed runs from past work. That creates uneven rooms and higher bills. Since Sky Clean Air started with duct cleaning and sealing, we are set up to fix airflow problems during your install.

What we look for

  • Leaks at joints that waste cooled or heated air into the attic with optional duct leakage testing
  • Restricted returns that choke airflow identified during a ductwork inspection
  • Kinks in flex duct that starve rooms which may need air duct repair
  • Dust buildup that hurts indoor air quality and benefits from duct sanitizing

Fixing these issues helps your new system run quietly and deliver the comfort you paid for. Balanced airflow matters and we can perform detailed airflow balancing.
