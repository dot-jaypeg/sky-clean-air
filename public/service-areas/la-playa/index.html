Our Duct Cleaning Process

We use a step by step approach that protects your home and gets real results. Curious how it works

  • Inspection and plan We look at supply and return ducts, registers, the air handler, and filter setup. We note access points, attic or crawlspace entry, and any HOA rules we need to follow.
  • Site prep We cover floors and furniture near vents. We set up containment and vacuum equipment. Parking on Shelter Island Drive can be tight. We plan for that before we arrive.
  • Strong negative pressure A high powered vacuum creates negative pressure in the duct system. That keeps dust moving toward the collection unit, not back into your rooms.
  • Agitation and source removal Rotary brushes and air whips loosen buildup on duct walls. We focus on elbows and branch lines where debris collects.
  • Register and grille cleaning We remove and clean vent covers. We wipe the boot area so dust does not sit right at the opening.
  • Optional coil cleaning and sanitizing If your evaporator coil is dirty, we can clean it. If odors are an issue, we can apply safe duct sanitizing. We talk it through before we start.
  • Final check and cleanup We confirm airflow and verify each run has been cleaned. We leave your rooms as clean as we found them.

Air Duct Cleaning in La Playa, CA often involves tight attic spaces and careful staging in older homes off Talbot and Canon Street. We plan around that so you get a smooth visit and clear results.

How Often Should La Playa Properties Clean Ducts and Vents

Coastal living speeds up dust and film inside ducts. Most La Playa homes do well with duct cleaning every 2 to 4 years. Homes with pets, allergies, or lots of guests may need it sooner.

Plan your routine

  • HVAC tune‑up twice a year
  • Filter changes every 1 to 3 months based on type and use
  • Dryer vent cleaning once a year for single family
  • Dryer vent cleaning every 6 months for long condo runs
  • Duct inspection yearly, cleaning as needed

During fire season or windy months, check filters more often. If the return grille looks grey, it is time to swap the filter.

A Closer Look At Problem and Solution Scenarios

The salty start up smell That whiff when the AC kicks on near Kellogg Beach often comes from film on duct walls and coils. Cleaning the ducts and the evaporator coil clears it. A UV light can help prevent it from returning.

Rooms that never quite cool Duct leaks in older homes on the slope above Shelter Island can send air into the attic instead of the bedroom. We test for leaks, seal the gaps, and balance the system. Consider duct leakage testing and air duct repair.

Dust that keeps coming back If you dust on Monday and see it again by Wednesday, your return path may be pulling attic air. We check return boxes, gaps around filters, and connections at the air handler. Thorough duct cleaning helps stop the cycle.

Dryer that takes two cycles Long vent runs in condos off Shelter Island Drive need cleaning more often. A booster fan might be the right call. We test airflow and propose the simplest fix that works, including dryer vent booster fan installation.

AC short cycling on windy days Winds can push debris into outdoor condensers. A coil cleaning and a check of the contactor and capacitor often clear this up.

Keep Your System Cleaner Between Visits

Simple habits make a big difference, especially near the water.

  • Change filters on schedule and upgrade to a media filter if your system supports it
  • Keep supply registers open and clear of rugs and furniture
  • Vacuum return grilles every month
  • Rinse the outdoor condenser gently with a hose from the inside out after high pollen or wind events
  • Run the fan in circulate mode for short periods to even out air if some rooms run hotter

Have pets or run a busy household near the marinas? Ask about filter options that catch more dander without choking airflow.
