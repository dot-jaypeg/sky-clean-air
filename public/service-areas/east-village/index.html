Why clean ducts help in our coastal city

  • Better airflow means rooms cool or heat faster
  • Less dust on shelves and floors
  • Lower strain on your AC or furnace and longer life
  • Fewer odors trapped in returns
  • Less moisture buildup that can lead to mold
  • Quieter operation with fewer rattles and whistles

East Village sees salt laden air near Harbor Drive and the trolley yards. That can corrode coils and stick to dust in your system. Routine cleaning, a proper filter, and an optional UV light at the coil keep things fresh and efficient.

Our step by step cleaning process

  • 1) Walkthrough and questions. We start with a short walkthrough. Where are your vents and returns, the air handler, and filter access? Any rooms that feel stale? Any pets or special concerns?
  • 2) System inspection. We check the ductwork condition, note any kinks, crushed flex, or sagging runs, and check access points. If we see signs of leaks, we can perform duct leakage testing.
  • 3) Protect your space. Drop cloths and register covers go down. We move a few small items if needed.
  • 4) Create negative pressure. A HEPA negative air machine connects to the trunk. This pulls debris safely out of the system.
  • 5) Agitate and collect. We run agitation tools down each run while the machine pulls dust to the collector. We clean each register, the return box, and accessible plenum sections.
  • 6) Optional sanitizing. If odors are present, we apply an EPA registered sanitizer inside the ducts. It helps with musty smells and light bacterial growth.
  • 7) Check airflow and seal leaks. We measure airflow at vents and look for obvious leaks. We can seal accessible joints to reduce waste.
  • 8) Final cleanup. We remove covers, wipe down registers, replace the filter if requested, and review what we found. You get tips for filter changes and schedule reminders if you want them.

Have a tight timeline before guests arrive after a Padres game? Tell us your window. We plan around it.

Dryer vent cleaning and safety for urban living

Laundry closets are common in East Village buildings along Island Avenue, K Street, and Broadway. Many of these vents run a long path to an exterior wall or roof. Lint clogs those runs over time. That raises drying times and creates a fire risk.

What we do

  • Check the termination cap for stuck flappers
  • Install or replace a booster fan if the run is very long
  • Reroute vents that take an inefficient path, when possible

Simple tips to keep air cleaner between visits

  • Change filters on time. Busy households and pet owners may need monthly filter changes. Others can go every two to three months.
  • Keep returns clear. Do not block returns with furniture or storage boxes.
  • Use your range hood. This reduces grease and odors in your ducts.
  • Vacuum vent covers. A small brush attachment works well.
  • Run the fan occasionally. Circulating air can even out temperatures and keep filters working.
  • Schedule seasonal tune ups. Spring for cooling and fall for heating keeps things reliable.

Troubleshooting common HVAC issues

AC runs but no cooling

Check the thermostat settings and filter. A clogged filter can freeze the coil. If the filter is clean and still no cooling, call us.

Hot and cold spots

You may need airflow balancing or a zoning setup. Leaky ducts can also cause it.

Short cycling

This can be a thermostat, a dirty coil, or a system size issue. We can diagnose and fix it.

Strange smells

Musty means moisture. Burning dust at the first furnace start of the season can be normal for a short time. Persistent odors need attention.

High bills

Look at duct leaks, dirty coils, poor insulation, or an aging system.
