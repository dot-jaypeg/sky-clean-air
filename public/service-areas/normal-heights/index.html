How Air Duct Cleaning Works In Your Space

Curious how we actually clean the ducts in a Normal Heights home or shop? Here is our step by step process.

Inspection

We check supply and return ducts, registers, the blower, coil area, and the plenum. We look for dust levels, signs of leaks, kinks, damaged flex runs, rodent droppings, or moisture staining. We also check access points in older homes that may need a small service port.

Prep

Floors get drop cloths. Registers come off and get covered during cleaning. We seal supply runs to focus suction and keep debris moving to the collector.

Negative Pressure

A powerful vacuum creates suction in the system so debris goes in one direction to the collector. We use HEPA filtration to trap fine particles.

Agitation and Dislodging

We feed specialty brushes and air whips through each run. This breaks loose stuck dust, dander, construction debris, and light surface mold. If we find a wet area or heavy growth, we will stop and show you. Moisture problems need to be fixed to avoid regrowth.

Clean the Blower and Plenum Area

We clean the blower compartment and wipe accessible surfaces, then reinstall panels with a tight fit.

Duct Sanitizing

If odors or bacteria are a concern, we apply a safe disinfecting agent through the system. This helps with lingering smells from pets, cooking, or past moisture.

Seal and Balance

We can seal gaps with mastic or approved tape, test for leakage, and adjust dampers to balance airflow to each room.

Final Walkthrough

We show you before and after photos and go over any fixes we recommend, along with simple filter tips.

This full approach is how we handle Air Duct Cleaning in Normal Heights, CA for both homes and businesses.

Practical Tips You Can Use Right Now

  • Check your filter every month during high use seasons
  • If you cannot see light through it, change it
  • Look at your supply vents
  • If you see dark lines, there may be leaks that pull in attic dust
  • Walk your home on a hot afternoon
  • Find rooms that feel off. Note whether doors slam shut when the system runs. This helps us spot pressure issues
  • Step outside and watch the dryer vent hood
  • If the flap barely opens during a cycle, the vent is likely restricted
  • Peek into the attic
  • If you see spots with thin insulation or exposed drywall, you are losing cool air
