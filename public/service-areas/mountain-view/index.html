Our Step‑By‑Step Duct Cleaning Process

Curious how we actually clean ducts? Here is a quick look.

  • 1) Inspection and setup. We check supply and return vents, inspect accessible ductwork, and look at the air handler. We protect floors and furniture and set up HEPA filtration.
  • 2) Powerful source removal. We connect a high‑capacity vacuum to the trunk line. Then we use rotary brushes and air whips to loosen debris so the vacuum pulls it out. Registers and grilles get cleaned as well.
  • 3) Coil and blower check. If your evaporator coil or blower wheel shows buildup, we can clean those surfaces. Clean coils improve heat transfer and reduce musty smells.
  • 4) Optional duct sanitizing. If odors or microbial growth are a concern, we apply an approved sanitizer inside the duct system. This is optional and we discuss it before we proceed.
  • 5) Final inspection and photos. We confirm airflow and take photos so you can see the difference.

Does your home need air balancing after cleaning? We can test and adjust to help each room get its fair share of airflow. Duct balancing can make a noticeable difference.

Common Comfort Problems We Solve

  • Hot and cold rooms Often caused by duct leaks, weak airflow, or poor layout. We seal leaks, add balancing dampers, and rework problem runs.
  • Dust and allergies We clean ducts, upgrade filters, and add a whole‑home air purifier or UV system to address fine particles and odors.
  • High energy bills Dirty coils, clogged filters, and leaky ducts make systems work harder. Cleaning, sealing, and tune‑ups help cut waste.
  • Noisy vents Whistling or rattling can point to undersized ducts or loose hardware. We diagnose and fix the root cause.
  • Short cycling or long run times We test system charge on AC units, check thermostats, and inspect airflow to get your equipment running the way it should.

Tips To Keep Your Air Fresher Between Visits

  • Change filters on schedule. If wildfire smoke rolls in, swap sooner.
  • Keep supply vents and returns clear of furniture.
  • Vacuum registers and grilles when you clean floors.
  • Use your range hood while cooking to cut grease in the air.
  • Run the fan on circulate for short periods to even out temps.
  • Close windows on high pollen days or during smoke events.
  • If you have pets, plan filter changes more often.

Small habits go a long way in Mountain View where air quality can shift fast.
