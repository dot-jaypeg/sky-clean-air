What You Get With Professional Air Duct Cleaning

Air ducts collect dust, pet hair, lint, insulation fibers, dander, and sometimes debris from past remodels. Over time that mess builds up. It restricts airflow, wears out your blower, and spreads irritants. Our professional cleaning improves indoor air, helps your system breathe, and can reduce those dust bunnies hiding under the couch.

Here is how we handle a typical cleaning

  • 1. Inspection and setup We review your layout, count registers, and find the main trunk lines. We protect flooring, then set up a high power negative pressure vacuum on the duct system.
  • 2. Source removal cleaning Using rotating brush tools, compressed air whips, and specialized nozzles, we agitate debris from each supply run and the return side while the vacuum pulls everything out to a sealed container outside.
  • 3. Registers and grilles We remove and clean registers and grilles. If you want them repainted, we can note that for a separate visit.
  • 4. Evaporator coil and blower check If needed, we inspect the blower and coil. Light debris is cleaned. Heavy buildup can be addressed during the same visit or scheduled as a separate service.
  • 5. Sanitizing on request If you have odor or past moisture issues, we can apply an EPA registered sanitizer inside the ductwork after the cleaning.
  • 6. System restart and test We start the system, check airflow, and confirm operation. We can finish with before and after photos.

Does air taste a bit stale after last fire season, or after a remodeling project off Austin Drive? A thorough cleaning goes a long way.

Our Duct Cleaning Process, Step by Step

  • Visual inspection and plan We map the system, locate returns, supplies, and key trunk lines.
  • Protect your home Floor covers, register guards, and corner protectors go in place.
  • Negative pressure setup A high capacity vacuum connects to your duct main. This creates strong suction that pulls debris outside.
  • Register by register cleaning We work through each vent line with brushes and air tools. We also clean the return side, which often holds the most dust.
  • Verify air handler and coil We check and, if agreed, clean the blower housing and coil face.
  • Sanitizing and deodorizing Optional treatment if you have odor, pets, or past moisture concerns.
  • Final checks We measure airflow, reinstall registers, and confirm everything runs smoothly.

Curious what comes out of a typical duct system? Picture a vacuum bag full of fine gray dust, some pet hair, maybe a few mysterious items like a toy car or a paperclip from 1998. The difference in airflow and cleanliness is noticeable.

How Often Should Ducts Be Cleaned

For most homes, every 3 to 5 years works well. If you have pets, allergies, a recent remodel, or past smoke exposure, every 2 to 3 years is smarter. We also suggest an annual system tune up and filter changes every 1 to 3 months, based on filter type and use.

Maintenance Tips You Can Do Right Now

  • Replace or wash filters on schedule A clogged filter is the fastest way to dirty ducts and stress your blower.
  • Keep supply and return vents open Closing vents can raise static pressure and create leaks.
  • Vacuum and wipe vents monthly Surface dust removal helps between cleanings.
  • Check around the air handler Look for water, rust, or odd noises. If you see something, call us.
  • During wildfire smoke days Set the system to recirculate, use a higher MERV media filter if your system supports it, and consider a whole home air purifier.
  • Plan seasonal tune ups AC in spring, heating in fall. Catch small issues early.

Why Professional Cleaning Beats DIY

Online videos make duct cleaning look simple, but proper results need strong negative pressure and the right tools. Shop vacs and air compressors push dust around and can make things worse.
