Our air duct cleaning process for Castle homes and businesses

We keep the process simple and neat. You get before and after photos, clear communication, and careful cleanup.

Here is how a typical job goes

  • Inspection and photos We check vents, returns, and main trunks. We note any damage, leaks, or growth.
  • Set up containment We protect floors and furniture. We place corner guards and lay down drop cloths.
  • Negative pressure We connect a high powered HEPA vacuum to pull dust and debris out of the system.
  • Agitation We use rotary brushes and air whips to break loose buildup inside the ducts, then capture it with our vacuum.
  • Supply and return cleaning We work through each supply run and return line, so the whole system is addressed.
  • Optional duct sanitizing We apply a safe disinfecting treatment to reduce bacteria and odors.
  • Grilles and registers We clean and wipe each vent cover and return grille.
  • Final test and photos We check airflow and provide after photos. You see what changed.

We aim for minimal disruption. If you operate a storefront near Broadway or a warehouse by the airport, we can schedule work before or after hours.

Add-ons that make a real difference

  • Duct sealing. Leaky ducts waste energy and pull dusty air from attics or crawlspaces. Sealing closes gaps and improves airflow.
  • Duct sanitizing. This reduces odors and microorganisms after cleaning.
  • Filter upgrades. A better media filter can cut down on new buildup inside ducts.
  • Airflow balancing. We adjust dampers and registers to smooth out hot and cold spots. Explore Duct Balancing options.

DIY tasks vs professional cleaning

There are helpful things you can handle between visits

  • Change filters on schedule. Most homes do well every 1 to 3 months.
  • Keep 2 to 3 feet clear around the outdoor unit. Trim bushes and pick up leaves.
  • Gently rinse the outdoor coil with a hose from the inside out if you can remove the top safely, or from the outside at a light angle. Do not blast it with a pressure washer.
  • Pour a small amount of white vinegar into the condensate drain line access to discourage algae. Once a month during heavy use is fine.
  • Keep supply and return grilles free of furniture and curtains.

What should you leave to a pro?

  • Coil cleaning with chemicals or tools
  • Blower wheel removal and cleaning
  • Electrical checks and capacitor testing
  • Replacing or recharging refrigerant
  • Deep cleaning inside tight air handlers
  • Ladder work on rooftop units

If you are handy, we are happy to show you simple upkeep while we work. Our goal is to help you keep the system clean longer.

Common AC issues we solve with cleaning

  • Stinky start-up smell. Often from a dirty coil or wet pan. Cleaning and drain treatment help.

  • High bills with normal weather. Dirty parts force long run times.
  • Water on the floor near the air handler. A clogged drain line or algae blockage usually causes this.
