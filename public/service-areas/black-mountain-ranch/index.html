Our air duct cleaning process

Clean ducts are about more than a quick vacuum at the vents. We follow a full system approach so the clean lasts.

1) System check and setup

  • Inspect supply and return trunks, registers, plenum, and blower compartment
  • Verify access panels and create service openings if needed, then seal them correctly at the end
  • Cover registers to control airflow during cleaning

2) Negative pressure and source removal

  • Connect a powerful negative air machine with HEPA filtration
  • Use rotary brushes and air whips to dislodge dust, lint, and debris
  • Pull waste into sealed containers for proper disposal

3) Return side and blower area

  • Clean return ducting and grills where dust collects fastest
  • Wipe and vacuum the blower compartment
  • Check the evaporator coil face and drain pan. If coil cleaning would help, we can add that service with your OK

4) Supply registers and grills

  • Remove and wash registers
  • Clean the duct near the opening where build up is common

5) Seal up and test

  • Seal access points and check for leakage
  • Run the system and confirm airflow

6) Optional services

  • Duct sanitizing for odors or microbes
  • Duct sealing for leakage
  • Airflow balancing adjustments

You’ll see before and after photos so you know exactly what changed.

Dryer vent cleaning and repair

Dryer vents do a lot of work in multi story homes around Black Mountain Ranch. Long vent runs snake through attics and out the roof. Lint build up makes cycles take longer, raises energy use, and can be a fire risk. Our team

  • Re routes vents that are too long or have too many turns

If towels need two cycles or your dryer feels hot to the touch, it’s time to check the vent.

Energy savings without guessing

Energy waste hides in two places

  • Leaky duct joints that pull hot attic air into the system
  • Low airflow from clogged coils and filters, which makes the system run longer

We test for leakage and measure airflow to target the real problem. Fixing leaks at boots and trunks, sealing the air handler cabinet, and swapping a poor filter rack can reduce runtime and cut dust.

Helpful tips between cleanings

Want to keep your ducts cleaner longer?

  • Change filters on time. If you can’t remember, set a reminder on your phone
  • Keep return grills clear of furniture and curtains
  • Vacuum floor registers when you do floors
  • Use kitchen and bath fans to pull moisture and odors out quickly
  • After a big dust event or wildfire smoke day, run the fan with a good filter for a while

If your filter looks dirty after one month every time, we can check for leakage pulling attic dust into returns.

How often should you clean ducts

Most homes in this area land between 3 to 5 years. You may need more often if
