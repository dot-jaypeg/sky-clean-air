What AC installation includes

AC installation is not just dropping in a box and flipping a switch. A proper install includes choices and steps that affect comfort, noise, and reliability.

Typical parts of an install may include

  • A site visit and a short conversation about comfort issues
  • Equipment selection based on your space and layout
  • Removal of old equipment when needed
  • Setting the outdoor unit on a stable pad or stand
  • Setting the indoor unit in an attic, closet, garage, or wall location depending on system type
  • Refrigerant line routing and insulation
  • Electrical connections and safety shutoffs
  • Condensate drain routing so water goes where it should
  • Airflow setup and basic balancing
  • Startup checks and operational walkthrough

Central air or ductless in La Jolla Shores

La Jolla Shores homes vary. You will see single family houses along Camino del Oro, condos near La Jolla Shores Drive, and properties tucked closer to Scripps Institution of Oceanography and UC San Diego edges. There is no single answer. The right choice depends on layout, access, and how you use rooms.

Central AC often makes sense when

  • You already have ducts in decent shape
  • You want even cooling across many rooms
  • Your indoor unit location is accessible for service

Ductless systems often make sense when

  • Your condo has limited space for ducts
  • You want room by room control
  • You are renovating and want minimal disruption to finished spaces

How we size an AC system

Sizing is about matching the system to your home’s real cooling demand. Too small can struggle on warmer days. Too large can short cycle and feel clammy.

  • Square footage and ceiling height
  • Insulation and attic conditions
  • Window size, shading, and orientation
  • Number of occupants and heat producing appliances
  • Duct layout and return air path
  • What rooms you actually use during the day

Coastal homes around La Jolla Shores can be tricky. The marine layer can keep mornings mild, then the sun pops out and the house warms quickly. The right setup accounts for those swings without chasing the thermostat all day.

Indoor placement and airflow

Indoor placement is where comfort is won or lost. A system can be new and still feel uneven if airflow is not handled well.

For central systems we pay attention to

  • Return air location and size since starved return air can make rooms feel stuffy
  • Supply register placement since poor placement can blast one corner and ignore the rest
  • Duct routing since long runs and tight bends can limit airflow

For ductless systems we look at

  • Wall head location for good throw and mixing
  • Clear paths that do not blow straight onto a bed or desk
  • Drain routing so water exits properly

If you already have ducts but airflow is weak

Weak airflow is common in older duct systems or remodels where walls moved and ducts did not. Before installing new equipment, we check the basics so you do not end up with a new system feeding old bottlenecks.

  • Filter location and fit since gaps can pull in dust and restrict airflow in odd ways
  • Return air sizing since a small return can choke the whole system
  • Duct condition including tears, crushed runs, and disconnected sections
  • Register sizing since tiny registers can make rooms feel undercooled
