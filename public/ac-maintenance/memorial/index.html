What AC maintenance includes

A solid maintenance visit is part cleaning, part inspection, and part performance check. The goal is to catch small problems before they turn into a no cool afternoon.

Here is what we typically cover during maintenance in Memorial depending on your system type and access.

  • Replace or clean the air filter if the correct size is on hand
  • Check thermostat operation and basic settings
  • Inspect the indoor blower area for dirt buildup
  • Inspect the evaporator coil condition and airflow restrictions
  • Check the outdoor condenser coil condition and clear light debris nearby
  • Inspect electrical connections for wear and heat marks
  • Measure temperature split and system cycling behavior
  • Check condensate drain line for clogs and signs of overflow
  • Listen for unusual motor or fan noises
  • Look for visible refrigerant line insulation damage

A quick real world example from Memorial. A homeowner told us it cools but it feels tired. The filter was packed, the drain line was starting to gum up, and the outdoor unit had leaves hugging the coil. After basic cleanup and checks, airflow improved and the system stopped short cycling.

Signs your AC is overdue for maintenance

Sometimes the system tells you plainly. Sometimes it is subtle. These are common signals we see in Memorial.

  • Airflow that feels weak from one or more vents
  • Rooms that cool unevenly
  • AC turning on and off more than it used to
  • Musty smell when the system starts
  • Water marks near the air handler or a damp smell around a closet unit
  • Outdoor unit sounding louder or rattly
  • Filter getting dirty fast even after replacement
  • Thermostat set lower than normal to feel comfortable

How to keep your AC in good shape between visits

A little attention goes a long way. You do not need to baby the system, but you can avoid common pitfalls.

  • Check the filter monthly during heavy use months
  • Keep at least two feet of clear space around the outdoor unit
  • Hose off light dust on the outdoor unit exterior when it is off if safe to do so
  • Make sure supply vents are open and not blocked by rugs or furniture
  • Pay attention to new sounds and smells and write down when they started
  • If you notice water near the indoor unit do not ignore it

Should you clean vents or ducts when you schedule maintenance. Sometimes yes and sometimes no. Maintenance focuses on the equipment. Duct cleaning is separate and makes sense in specific situations such as post remodel dust or visible debris at registers. What to do before the technician arrives

A couple quick steps can make the visit easier and faster.

  • Make a clear path to the thermostat and indoor unit
  • Unlock gates to the outdoor unit if applicable
  • Keep pets secure so doors can be opened safely
  • Note any recent changes new noises or comfort problems
  • Know where the electrical panel is just in case access is needed
