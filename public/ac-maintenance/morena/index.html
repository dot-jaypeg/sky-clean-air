What does AC maintenance include

A good maintenance visit is part cleaning, part inspection, part problem spotting. The goal is to catch the small stuff before it turns into the why is the house 82 degrees moment.

Typical AC maintenance steps include

  • Thermostat check and basic operation test
  • Filter condition check and airflow check
  • Indoor unit inspection for dust buildup and drainage issues
  • Outdoor unit inspection for debris, coil condition, and fan operation
  • Electrical visual checks for worn connections and signs of overheating
  • Refrigerant line inspection for insulation damage and oily residue that can hint at leaks
  • Condensate drain check to reduce clogs and water overflow
  • Temperature split check to see how the system is performing right now

A quick real life example from Morena. A resident near Tecolote Canyon told us, It cools, but it feels lazy. After basic checks, the issue was restricted airflow from a loaded filter and dust buildup at the return. Cleaning and restoring airflow made the system feel more responsive.

What are the signs your AC needs maintenance

Sometimes the system is working but still asking for help. Watch for these common signs.

  • Airflow feels weaker at some vents
  • Rooms cool unevenly, like the bedroom is fine but the living room is warm
  • The system turns on and off more often than usual
  • Musty smell when the AC starts
  • New rattling, buzzing, or clicking sounds
  • Water around the indoor unit or dripping near a drain line
  • Outdoor unit looks clogged with leaves, lint, or dirt
  • Higher indoor humidity than normal

A quick test you can do. Put your hand at a supply vent you use often. If airflow feels lower than you remember, it is worth checking the filter and scheduling a maintenance visit.

What should you do before the appointment

You do not need to prep like you are hosting a dinner party. A little access goes a long way.

Before we arrive, it helps if you can

  • Replace or locate your current filter if you have a spare handy
  • Clear a small path to the indoor unit, often in a closet, hallway, or garage
  • Make sure the outdoor unit has a couple feet of space around it
  • Note any patterns like it struggles around 3 pm, the guest room is always warmer, or it makes a chirp when it shuts off

If you have had recent remodeling, mention it. Drywall dust has a way of traveling like it pays rent.

A quick guide to filters in Morena

Filters matter, and not just for dust. They shape airflow. The wrong fit can let air slip around the edges, which is like wearing a raincoat with the zipper open.

Basic filter tips

  • Check the filter monthly during heavy use seasons
  • Replace when it looks loaded or when airflow seems reduced
  • Make sure the filter size matches the slot
  • Install it in the correct airflow direction
