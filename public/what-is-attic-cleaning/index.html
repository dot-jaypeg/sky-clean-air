What Is Attic Cleaning, and When Does Your Home Need It?

Updated October 21, 2026 3 min read By the Sky Clean Air team

The short answer

Attic cleaning is the removal of dirty or contaminated insulation, rodent droppings, nests and debris from your attic, followed by sanitizing, sealing entry points and usually installing fresh insulation. Homes need it most after a rodent infestation, water or smoke damage, or when old insulation has become compacted and dirty.

Most people rarely look in their attic, but in Southern California it's a busy space. The furnace or air handler often lives there, ducts run through it and insulation covers the floor. When rats, water or smoke get in, the attic can affect your comfort, energy bills and the air you breathe.

What does attic cleaning include?

A full attic cleaning usually follows these steps:

  1. Inspection. Check the insulation, rodent activity, entry points, ducts, wiring and any moisture.
  2. Prep and protection. Protect the living space, the attic access and the path to the truck.
  3. Insulation removal. Remove old or contaminated insulation with a commercial vacuum system that sends it straight into bags outside.
  4. Debris and waste removal. Clear droppings, nests, dead rodents and debris.
  5. Sanitizing and deodorizing. Treat the attic surfaces to reduce odors and contamination.
  6. Sealing. Close gaps and openings where pests got in, and air seal leaks between the house and the attic.
  7. New insulation. Install fresh insulation to the right depth.

When does an attic need cleaning?

  • After rats, mice or other animals have lived in the attic
  • When insulation has droppings, urine stains or nests
  • After a roof leak soaks the insulation
  • After a nearby fire leaves smoke odor or soot
  • When insulation is old, compacted or thin and no longer insulates well
  • Before or after a remodel that disturbed the attic
  • When there's a persistent odor coming from the ceiling or vents

Why does contaminated insulation matter?

Rodent waste and nesting don't just smell. They can carry bacteria and viruses, and the odor and scent trails can attract new rodents. Contaminated insulation also loses much of its ability to keep heat in or out. Flattened, wet or shredded insulation means your heating and cooling work harder.

The attic also connects to your living space more than you might think. Gaps around light fixtures, ceiling fans, plumbing and the attic hatch let attic air into the house. Leaky return ducts in the attic can pull that air straight into your HVAC system.

What does attic cleaning have to do with your HVAC system?

In most local homes, the furnace or air handler and much of the ductwork sit in the attic. Rodents nest near the warm equipment and chew on flexible ducts. A torn return duct can pull dust, insulation fibers and odors from the attic and blow them through the house. That's why attic cleaning and a duct inspection often go together. Fixing one without the other can leave the problem half solved.

How long does attic cleaning take?

It depends on the size of the attic, how much insulation needs to come out and how much damage there is. Many homes can be done in one to two days. Larger attics or jobs with heavy contamination can take longer. You can usually stay home during the work, since the crew works from the attic and outside.

Can you clean an attic yourself?

Small jobs, like removing a few stored boxes or checking for signs of pests, are fine. Full insulation removal is different. It involves working in tight, hot spaces, walking only on framing, handling contaminated material and moving large volumes of insulation. Disturbing droppings without protection can put particles in the air. A professional crew has the vacuum equipment, protective gear and experience to do it cleanly.

What should you do after the attic is cleaned?

Walk the outside of the house too. Look for gaps at the roofline, damaged vent screens and branches touching the roof, since those are how most pests got in the first time.

A clean attic stays clean when the cause is fixed. That means:

  • Keeping entry points sealed and checking them once in a while
  • Trimming trees away from the roof
  • Fixing roof leaks quickly
  • Checking attic ducts for damage, since rodents often chew through them

If your ducts were damaged or contaminated, cleaning or replacing them is the next step. See our attic cleaning and insulation services and our air duct services for more.

Common Questions

Is attic cleaning the same as insulation replacement?
They often go together. Attic cleaning removes the old insulation and contamination, and insulation replacement installs new material. Some homes only need new insulation added on top, while others need full removal first.
Will attic cleaning get rid of rats?
Cleaning removes the mess they left behind and the scent that attracts more. The rats themselves need to be removed and entry points sealed so they can't come back.
Does new insulation lower energy bills?
Adequate insulation reduces how much heat moves between your attic and living space, so the HVAC system works less. Results depend on the home, existing insulation and how the system is used.
How do I know if my attic insulation is contaminated?
Look for droppings, tunnels, nests, stains and flattened areas, or notice a strong odor. An inspection can confirm how much of the insulation is affected.
