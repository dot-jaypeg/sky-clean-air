How do I know if my ducts actually need cleaning

Some people schedule cleaning after a move. Others wait until dust becomes a household member. If you are unsure, look for signs you can verify without tools.

Signs that often point to duct buildup include these.

  • Dust puffs out of a vent when the system starts
  • You remove a vent cover and see a gray layer right inside
  • Musty smell from vents that is stronger at startup
  • After remodeling, fine dust lingers longer than expected
  • You have a new pet, or your pet has discovered shedding as a hobby
  • A filter gets dirty very quickly compared to normal
  • You see debris around the return grille

Will air duct cleaning improve airflow

Sometimes. Not always. Airflow is affected by several factors.

Duct cleaning may help airflow if these issues are present.

  • Buildup is restricting a return path
  • Debris is lodged near a takeoff or boot
  • Grilles are clogged with dust and lint

Airflow may not change much if these conditions are driving the problem.

  • The duct layout has tight turns or long runs
  • The system is undersized or aging
  • Registers are closed or blocked by furniture
  • There are duct leaks or disconnected sections

What is the difference between supply vents and return vents, and why does it matter

Supply vents deliver air into rooms. Return vents pull air back to the system to be filtered and conditioned again. If returns are dirty or restricted, the system can struggle.

A quick way to remember it is this. Supplies blow. Returns pull.

Many homes in Teralta East have a main return in a hallway or living area and supplies in bedrooms. If the return grille is coated in dust, or if a filter slot is letting air bypass the filter, the system can pull dust into places it should not. After cleaning, what should I do to keep things cleaner
