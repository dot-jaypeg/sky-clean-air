How to know if your ducts need cleaning

You do not need a dramatic horror movie moment when you remove a vent cover. Most signs are smaller and more annoying than alarming.

Look for patterns like

  • Dust puffs from vents when the system starts
  • You wipe vent covers and they are dirty again quickly
  • Certain rooms smell off when air kicks on
  • You see visible buildup just inside the vent opening
  • You had drywall work, flooring work, or sanding and the HVAC ran during it
  • You recently bought the home and do not know the system history

A quick check you can do today

  • Turn the HVAC off
  • Remove one supply vent cover and one return vent cover
  • Use a flashlight to look just inside
  • If you see a thick mat of dust or debris right at the entry, that is a sign there may be more further in

No need to stick a shop vacuum hose in there and hope for the best. That usually turns into a mess and leaves the main trunk lines untouched.

How often should ducts be cleaned

There is no single schedule that fits every property. It depends on use, filters, pets, and whether you have had renovations or smoke exposure.

You may consider cleaning more often if

  • You have multiple shedding pets
  • Someone in the home is sensitive to dust
  • You recently completed remodeling
  • The home sat vacant for a long time
  • You notice recurring dust at vents despite good filter habits

You may be able to wait longer if

  • Filters are changed consistently
  • The home stays closed up most days
  • There are no pets
  • There has been no recent construction activity

If you are not sure, a quick conversation about your setup usually clears it up. You can also look at our broader Service areas if you manage properties outside the valley.

Most common vent and duct issues we find

We see a handful of repeat offenders. They are not glamorous, but they are familiar.

Common findings

  • Supply vents with a thick ring of dust right behind the grille
  • Return vents acting like a vacuum for pet hair
  • Debris left behind from past construction or painting
  • Duct joints that have small gaps where dust can enter
  • Vent covers that are clogged or painted shut

Sometimes the fix is not just cleaning. Sometimes it is also simple housekeeping around the returns or updating filter habits. We will tell you what we think is going on, in plain language.

A quick guide to supply vents vs return vents

Vent type What it does What you might notice when it is dirty
Supply vent Pushes conditioned air into rooms Dust blowing out and weaker airflow
Return vent Pulls air back to the HVAC system Dusty grille and more dust near the vent

If you only clean what you can see on the supply side, the return can still pull dust into the system. It is a loop.
