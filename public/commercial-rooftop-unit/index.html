What Is a Commercial Rooftop Unit

A Commercial Rooftop Unit is a self contained HVAC system placed on a curb on your roof. Inside that cabinet you will find the supply fan return fan cooling section heating section filters control board and often an economizer for free cooling. The unit connects to your duct system through a roof curb and sends conditioned air through the building. Learn more about the concept on this rooftop unit overview.

Why do so many San Diego buildings use rooftop units

  • They save indoor space for people and products
  • Service techs can access the unit without walking through your business
  • Units are modular so upgrades and swaps are faster
  • Economizers let you use cool coastal air in spring and fall which helps cut power costs

Why San Diego Buildings Rely On RTUs

San Diego has microclimates. A shop in La Jolla fights salt air. A warehouse in Otay Mesa deals with dusty winds. A gym in Pacific Beach sees heavy use and sweat load after 5 p.m. A medical office in Kearny Mesa needs steady temperature low noise and clean air. A proper Commercial Rooftop Unit setup answers all of that.

  • Marine layer mornings and sunny afternoons demand smart controls that adjust quickly
  • Coastal zones need coil coatings and hardware that resist corrosion
  • Inland areas get hotter in summer so variable speed fans and higher cooling capacity matter
  • Many cities in the county follow Title 24 energy rules so economizers demand control ventilation and better fan control help meet code and lower bills

Special Considerations in San Diego

Our city and county throw a few curveballs at rooftop units. We plan for those from day one.

Coastal corrosion

Salt air attacks coils and fasteners. We use coated coils sealed electrical boxes and stainless hardware on coastal sites. We also suggest rinse visits during peak salt season which can be part of AC maintenance.

Marine layer and economizers

Cool mornings are perfect for free cooling. We tune outdoor air sensors so you use the breeze without bringing in foggy damp air that can cause sticky rooms.

Wildfire smoke days

We set filters and outside air controls to protect indoor air quality. This can include temporary changes to outside air intake and filter ratings with a plan to return to normal once air clears.

Rooftop access and cranes

Downtown Mission Valley or UTC can be busy. We handle crane permits flagging and timing so neighbors and tenants stay happy.

Solar arrays

We work around solar panels and coordinate with your solar provider when needed so wires and arrays remain safe.

Older buildings

We often find old curbs and duct transitions. Curb adapters and smart sheet metal work keep projects on track without rebuilding the entire roof opening.

What is a commercial rooftop unit?

A commercial rooftop unit is a type of heating and cooling system installed on the roof of a commercial building to control indoor temperature and air quality.

How do I know if my business needs a rooftop unit?

If your business requires consistent indoor comfort and efficient temperature control, a rooftop unit may be a good option.

What types of buildings can use rooftop units?

Rooftop units are commonly used in office buildings, retail stores, restaurants, warehouses, and schools.

Regular maintenance, typically at least twice a year, is recommended to keep your unit running smoothly.

What are the benefits of installing a rooftop unit?
