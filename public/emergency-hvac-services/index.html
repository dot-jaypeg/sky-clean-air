What counts as an HVAC emergency?

Not every hiccup is urgent, but some problems should be handled right away to protect your home, your equipment, and your family’s comfort.

Cooling emergencies

  • AC not turning on and indoor temps rising
  • Warm air blowing when the thermostat is set to cool
  • Frequent tripping of the breaker for the AC
  • Ice on the refrigerant lines or outdoor unit
  • Burning smell or electrical odor from the air handler
  • Water leaking from the indoor unit or ceiling
  • Loud banging, screeching, or grinding noises

Heating emergencies

  • Furnace not firing on a cold night
  • Heat pump stuck in cooling or not heating well
  • Repeated short cycling and safety lockouts
  • Burning or electrical smells
  • Strange noises from the blower or burner area
  • Carbon monoxide alarm sounding
  • Pilot will not stay lit or igniter clicks but no flame

Airflow and duct problems

  • Almost no airflow in parts of the home
  • Sudden rise in dust, allergies, or musty odors
  • A collapsed, disconnected, or blocked duct
  • Condensation or water dripping from supply vents

If you smell gas, leave the building right away and call 911 or SDG&E. Once your space is safe, we can fix the HVAC issue that caused the concern.

What to try before you call

Want a quick win? A few safe steps might get you going or at least give us clues so we fix it faster.

  • Check the thermostat. Set it to cool or heat and at least 3 degrees beyond the current room temperature. Replace the batteries if it has them.
  • Look at the breaker panel. Reset any tripped breaker for the furnace, air handler, or condenser. If it trips again, stop and call us.
  • Inspect the air filter. A clogged filter can choke airflow and freeze coils. Replace the filter if it is dirty.
  • Peek at the outdoor unit. Remove leaves or debris around the condenser. Keep at least 2 feet of clearance.
  • Check for ice. If you see ice on the refrigerant lines, turn the system off and set the fan to On to help thaw. Running a frozen system can cause damage.
  • Look for water. If you see water near the indoor unit, turn off the system. A clogged condensate drain can overflow and damage ceilings or floors.

If there is a burning smell, gas odor, or visible sparking, shut off power at the breaker, leave the area, and call for help. Safety first.

Common emergency fixes we handle today

You do not need to know every part name. Still, it helps to understand what might be going on. Here are the usual suspects we see across San Diego.

AC repairs

  • Failed capacitor or contactor that keeps the outdoor unit from starting
  • Burned blower motor or seized bearings
  • Refrigerant leak that caused low pressure and warm air
  • Clogged condensate drain that drips through ceilings in Mission Valley condos
  • Frozen evaporator coil from low airflow or low refrigerant
  • Faulty thermostat or failed control board
  • Dirty condenser coil after a dusty Santa Ana week

Heat pump fixes

  • Reversing valve stuck and not switching between heat and cool
  • Defrost control issues on chilly, damp mornings in coastal areas
  • Low refrigerant leading to weak heat output
  • Outdoor fan motor failure or iced over outdoor unit

Furnace repairs

  • Igniter or flame sensor failure
  • Dirty burners causing rough start and noisy flames
  • Faulty inducer motor and pressure switch errors
  • Limit switch trips from clogged filter or blocked vents
  • Intermittent pilot issues on older systems

Duct and airflow repairs

  • Disconnected or crushed flexible duct in the attic
  • Return duct leaks pulling dusty attic air into the home
  • Closed or blocked registers causing hot and cold spots
  • Blower wheel packed with dust and pet hair, cutting airflow

Thermostats and controls

  • Older thermostats with failing relays or sensors
  • Wi‑Fi thermostat settings or schedule conflicts after power blips
  • Bad wiring connections at the furnace or air handler

Electrical and safety

  • Shorted wires from rodents in attics or crawl spaces
  • Breakers tripping from overloaded circuits
  • Loose lugs or burned connections inside the disconnect

We fix the cause, not just the symptom, so you are not calling again next week. If needed, we can also perform HVAC Troubleshooting to track down intermittent issues.

Safety first, always

HVAC issues mix electricity, gas, water, and moving parts. A few reminders help keep everyone safe.

  • Gas smell means get out right away and call 911 or SDG&E
  • If you see smoke or sparks, cut power at the breaker and step away
  • Do not reach into equipment while it is powered
  • Keep children and pets away from the work area
  • Water near electrical gear is risky, so shut off power and call us

Tell us what you see and smell. Those details guide our next steps and keep the visit efficient.
