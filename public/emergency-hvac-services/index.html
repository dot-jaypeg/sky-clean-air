Urgent HVAC Service in San Diego & Orange County

If it's urgent, call first

Don't book online or send an email when your system has quit. Call 619-304-8822. A phone call is the quickest way for us to understand what's going on, tell you whether it's safe to keep the system running, and find the earliest slot we have.

Our office hours are Monday through Friday, 8am to 6pm, and Saturday, 8am to 3pm. Within those hours, we move urgent calls up the list and get you in the same day when we can. We won't promise an arrival time we can't keep, but we'll be straight with you about when we can be there.

First, is anyone in danger?

Some situations aren't an HVAC call. They're a safety call:

  • You smell gas. Leave the house, then call 911 or your gas utility from outside. Don't flip switches on the way out.
  • Your carbon monoxide alarm is going off. Get everyone outside and call 911.
  • You see smoke or sparks from the unit or the electrical panel. Turn off power at the breaker if you can do it safely, and get out.

Once everyone is safe and emergency responders have cleared the house, call us to find and fix the cause.

What counts as urgent

You know your household best, but these are the calls we treat as a priority:

  • No cooling during a heat wave, especially with babies, older family members, or anyone with a health condition at home
  • No heat on cold nights
  • A burning or electrical smell that doesn't go away
  • Water leaking from an attic air handler or through a ceiling
  • Loud grinding, banging or screeching
  • A breaker that trips again as soon as you reset it

Heat is the big one here. When Santa Ana conditions push temperatures up across the county, inland neighborhoods like El Cajon, Santee, Escondido, Poway, Lake Forest and Mission Viejo can get hot enough that a dead AC is a genuine health concern. If that's your situation, tell us when you call.

What you can do right now

While you wait, a few steps can keep damage down and sometimes get the system running again:

  1. Check the thermostat. Make sure it's set to heat or cool and that the screen is on. Replace the batteries if it's blank.
  2. Check the breakers. Reset a tripped breaker once. If it trips again, leave it off.
  3. Swap the filter if it's clogged. A choked filter can make coils freeze and systems shut down.
  4. If there's ice on the lines or coil, turn cooling off and run just the fan to let it thaw. Running the AC while it's iced can damage the compressor.
  5. If water is leaking, turn the system off, put a bucket or towels down, and move anything valuable out of the way.
  6. Keep the house bearable. In heat, close blinds on sunny windows and run ceiling fans. In cold, close off unused rooms and use safe, approved space heaters.

When you call, tell us what you've tried and what you noticed. It helps the tech arrive with the right parts.

What happens when we get there

An urgent visit follows the same steps as any HVAC repair, just with more urgency. The tech diagnoses the problem, explains it plainly, and gives you a written, upfront price before starting work. Often we can make the fix on the spot. If a part has to be ordered, we'll tell you what that means for timing and whether there's a safe temporary way to get you some heating or cooling in the meantime.

If your system is older and this is a major failure, we'll be honest about whether it's worth repairing. That's a hard conversation to have in the middle of a heat wave, so we'll give you the options without pushing. Our replacement page explains how we think it through.

Where we can help

We send techs across San Diego County, from San Ysidro and Chula Vista through Kearny Mesa, where our office is, and up to Fallbrook, and across Orange County from San Clemente to Brea. How soon we can reach you depends on the day's schedule and where you are, and we'll give you an honest estimate on the phone.

What to tell us on the phone

The more we know before we arrive, the better the odds we fix it on the first trip. When you call, it helps to have:

  • Your address and the best number to reach you
  • What the system is doing, or not doing, and when it started
  • Whether it's heating, cooling or both that's affected
  • Any smells, sounds, water or error codes on the thermostat
  • Roughly how old the system is, if you know
  • Where the equipment is: attic, closet, garage or roof

Avoiding the next one

Most HVAC emergencies start as small problems nobody noticed: a weakening capacitor, a slowly clogging drain line, a coil caked with dust from the last fire season. A yearly tune-up catches a lot of that. Our basic tune-up is currently $50. See our specials. It's a lot less stressful than a breakdown on the hottest weekend of the year.

Emergency HVAC Services: Frequently Asked Questions

Are you open 24/7?

No. Our hours are Monday through Friday, 8am to 6pm, and Saturday, 8am to 3pm. During those hours, we prioritize urgent calls and get you in the same day whenever the schedule allows.

How fast can you get here?

It depends on the day's schedule and your location. We don't quote a fixed response time, but we'll give you an honest estimate when you call, and same-day visits are often possible.

Does urgent service cost more?

Ask when you call and we'll tell you. Either way, you'll get a written, upfront price before any work begins.

Should I turn my system off while I wait?

If it's leaking, smells like burning, is making loud mechanical noises, has ice on it, or keeps tripping the breaker, yes. If it's just running weakly, it's usually fine to leave it on, but check the filter.

My AC is out and it's over 100 degrees inland. What should I do?

Call us right away and mention the heat and anyone at higher risk in the home. In the meantime, keep blinds closed, run fans, drink water, and consider spending the hottest part of the day somewhere cool. If anyone shows signs of heat illness, call 911.

Do you handle urgent calls for businesses?

Yes. See our commercial emergency HVAC page, or call and let us know it's a commercial property.
