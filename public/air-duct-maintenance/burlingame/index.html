What air duct maintenance really means

Think of your ductwork like the roadway for your heated and cooled air. If it is cluttered with dust, construction debris, pet hair, or loose insulation, airflow can drop and your vents can start talking back with whistling or rattling.

Air duct maintenance can include

  • Visual inspection of supply and return ducts
  • Checking vent covers and returns for buildup and blockage
  • Identifying disconnected ducts, crushed flex duct, or loose joints
  • Noting moisture signs near boots, registers, or in nearby framing
  • Cleaning duct interiors when buildup is present
  • Recommending filter type and change timing based on your setup
  • Checking basic airflow balance room to room

A quick story we hear a lot in Burlingame goes like this. We remodeled the hallway, and now the bedroom is always dusty. Construction dust loves duct systems. It finds a way in, then shows up for months like an uninvited guest.

Filters and what you can do between visits

Filters are the first line of defense, but they are often misunderstood. A filter that is too restrictive for a system can reduce airflow. A filter that is too loose can let more debris through.

We help you match filter choices to your system and household needs. That includes

  • Checking the filter slot size and fit
  • Noting bypass gaps where air goes around the filter
  • Recommending a change routine based on dust load, pets, and run time
  • Reminding you that I changed it last year is not a schedule

You can keep things under control with a few simple habits

  • Change filters on a consistent schedule that matches your home dust level
  • Keep return grilles clear of furniture baskets and temporary storage
  • Vacuum around supply vents and returns during normal cleaning
  • If you remodel cover registers and return openings during dusty work
  • Do not run the HVAC fan nonstop during sanding or drywall work unless filtration is upgraded
