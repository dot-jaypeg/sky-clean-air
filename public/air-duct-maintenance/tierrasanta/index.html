What counts as air duct maintenance

Air duct maintenance is more than a quick vent vacuum. Done right, it is a set of steps that help keep the air pathway and nearby components in workable shape.

Typical air duct maintenance includes

  • A walk through of supply vents and return grilles
  • Checking visible duct connections in accessible areas
  • Looking for common issues like loose fittings, dusty returns, and clogged registers
  • Cleaning inside the ductwork where buildup is present
  • Addressing the areas that often collect the most debris
  • Return side ducts
  • The air handler area
  • Vent boot openings
  • Replacing or installing filters if you provide them, and confirming fit and direction
  • Notes on what we found and what to watch next

How to tell if the problem is ducts or something else

Not every comfort issue is a duct issue. Sometimes it is thermostat placement, insulation, closed interior doors, or a filter that is overdue.

Clues that point toward duct related trouble

  • Certain vents are weak while others feel fine

  • Dust collects heavily around a specific supply vent
  • A room at the end of the run feels consistently off
  • You see disconnected or sagging duct in accessible areas

Clues that might point elsewhere

  • Temperature swings that follow sun exposure and window heat
  • A system that short cycles on and off
  • Issues that started right after a thermostat change
  • Consistent comfort problems only when doors are closed
