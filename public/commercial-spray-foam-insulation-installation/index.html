What Is Spray Foam Insulation

Spray polyurethane foam expands on contact sealing gaps and filling odd spaces. It sticks to many building surfaces and creates a continuous air seal where batts and blown in products fall short. Two main types are used in commercial spaces.

  • Closed cell spray foam
    • High R-value per inch
    • Adds rigidity to assemblies
    • Resists water and vapor and can act as a vapor retarder
    • Ideal for underside of roof decks metal buildings and areas with moisture risk
  • Open cell spray foam
    • Lower R-value per inch than closed cell
    • Great for sound absorption and air sealing inside walls and ceilings
    • Often used in offices schools and tenant build outs

We match the foam type and thickness to your needs. The right choice depends on your building’s use current envelope budget and future plans. If your goals include tighter ducts and cleaner air consider pairing with Commercial Attic Air Sealing or Commercial Air Duct Cleaning.

Spray Foam For Flat Roofs And Metal Roofs

San Diego has many low slope and metal roof buildings. Those roofs get intense sun and wide temperature swings. The underside of the deck can heat up fast then drop cool as the marine layer rolls in. This cycle can create condensation on metal and reduce comfort inside.

Closed cell foam under the roof deck helps in three ways.

  • Raises the R-value of the roof assembly
  • Seals gaps and seams where hot air and dust sneak in and pairs well with Commercial Attic Air Sealing
  • Acts as a moisture retarder to reduce condensation

The result is cooler summers inside the building less risk of drips and more stable conditions for workers and stored goods.

If your roof has existing issues we can coordinate with your roofer. Spray foam inside the building supports the system but does not replace roof repairs above the deck. Many owners pair interior foam with efficiency upgrades later for added savings.

Walls Partitions And Ceilings

Interior walls and demising partitions can leak sound and air between suites. Open cell foam helps absorb sound and reduce cross smells from kitchens or shop areas. In offices this makes meeting rooms and phone rooms more private. In retail centers this keeps odors and smoke from drifting between tenants.

Ceilings above drop tiles often hide big air gaps. Spray foam at the deck can seal those gaps and cut load on your HVAC. You get fewer drafts and cleaner indoor air because unfiltered outside air is not bypassing your system. For even distribution ask about Commercial Duct Balancing.

Moisture Control And Coastal Conditions

Close to the ocean moisture finds weak spots fast. Morning fog and marine layer can raise humidity inside the building through tiny openings. Later in the day sun heats the roof pulls air through cracks and creates pressure shifts. That cycle invites condensation on metal parts and inside walls.

Closed cell foam reduces that movement. It blocks vapor drive seals pathways and keeps surfaces warmer than the dew point. That cuts the chance of dripping staining and damage to finish materials. If you have water marks on the underside of your roof or rusty fasteners on purlins spray foam can stop the cycle that causes those problems.

Commercial Spray Foam Insulation: Frequently Asked Questions

What is commercial spray foam insulation?

Commercial spray foam insulation is a material sprayed onto walls, ceilings, and other surfaces to help buildings stay comfortable and energy efficient.

What are the main benefits of spray foam insulation for businesses?

It improves energy efficiency, reduces noise, helps control moisture, and makes interiors more comfortable.

Where can spray foam insulation be used in a commercial building?

It can be applied to walls, roofs, ceilings, and floors in offices, warehouses, retail spaces, and more.

How long does spray foam insulation take to install?

Installation times vary based on project size, but many jobs can be completed in a day or two.

Is spray foam insulation safe for indoor environments?

Will spray foam insulation help lower my energy bills?
