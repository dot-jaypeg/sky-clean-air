Is it time to replace your AC or can it be repaired

Some systems just need a fix. Others keep asking for one more repair until you feel like you are funding their retirement. If you are still weighing options, start with AC Repair in Del Cerro, CA and compare it to replacement based on system condition.

Replacement starts making sense when you notice patterns like these.

  • Repairs are becoming frequent or the same issue keeps coming back
  • The system struggles to cool bedrooms while the living room feels fine
  • You hear new sounds like rattling, buzzing, or a fan that never sounds settled
  • The AC runs longer but the home still feels warm or sticky
  • Airflow is weak at some vents, especially in back bedrooms or additions
  • You see ice on refrigerant lines or water where it should not be
  • Your system uses older refrigerant types that are harder to manage over time

What does AC replacement include

AC replacement is more than swapping a metal box and calling it a day. A proper replacement looks at the full system so the new equipment can actually deliver comfort through your ducts and vents. If airflow is part of the complaint, reviewing Air Duct Repair in Del Cerro, CA alongside equipment choices is often helpful.

Typical AC replacement work includes

  • Reviewing your current system type and how your home is laid out
  • Confirming what equipment is being replaced

  • Start up testing and temperature split checks

What should you do before and after your new AC is installed

A few small steps can make installation smoother and help your system start off clean.

Before installation

  • Clear a path to the furnace closet or attic access
  • Move cars away from the outdoor unit area if space is tight
  • Note any rooms that are always hotter or colder
  • Tell us about any odd sounds, odors, or past water leaks

After installation

  • Set a reasonable thermostat schedule and avoid constant fiddling
  • Check that supply vents are open and not blocked by furniture
  • Replace or clean filters on a consistent schedule
  • Watch for signs like unusual water around the indoor unit
  • If something feels off, say it early while it is easy to adjust
