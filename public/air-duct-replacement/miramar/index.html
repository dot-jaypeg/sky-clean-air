When does ductwork actually need replacement

Some duct issues can be sealed or repaired. Others are like trying to patch a garden hose that has been chewed in five places. Replacement tends to make sense when the duct material or layout is the main problem.

Common reasons we recommend replacing ducts in Miramar properties include

  • Crushed or kinked flexible duct in attics or above drop ceilings
  • Old duct board that is shedding fibers or falling apart
  • Rusted metal duct sections near moisture sources
  • Loose, leaky joints that have been fixed repeatedly with tape
  • Ducts that are the wrong size for the equipment and rooms
  • Runs that were routed in a way that starves certain rooms
  • Persistent odors that trace back to contaminated duct materials
  • Rodent damage, nesting, or heavy debris that keeps returning

A quick reality check. If you have a newer HVAC unit but the building still feels uneven, the ductwork is often the silent culprit. The system can only push what the duct network allows.

Supply ducts versus return ducts

Supply ducts deliver conditioned air to rooms. Return ducts pull air back to the system to be filtered and conditioned again. Both matter, and return problems are often sneaky. For background on how HVAC works in general, you can read the Wikipedia page on heating, ventilation, and air conditioning.

Supply duct problems often show up as

  • Weak airflow at registers
  • Hot and cold spots
  • Noisy vents

Return duct problems often show up as

  • Rooms that feel stuffy even with the fan on
  • Doors that slam or are hard to close when the system runs
  • Dusty air or odors that seem to come from somewhere
  • Filter loading up fast

In a lot of Miramar commercial spaces, returns are modified during tenant changes. A wall gets moved, a ceiling grid changes, and the return path becomes an afterthought. We look at how air gets back to the unit, not just how it leaves.
