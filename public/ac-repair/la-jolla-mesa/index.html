What AC repair covers and what it does not

AC repair focuses on solving a specific problem that is keeping your system from performing normally. That can mean replacing a failed part, correcting an electrical issue, addressing a drain problem, or restoring proper airflow. AC repair is different from AC Replacement in La Jolla Mesa, CA and different from routine AC Maintenance. If a system is old, damaged, or unsafe to run, we will say so plainly and talk through options, but the visit still starts with diagnosis, not assumptions.

Common AC repair tasks

  • Diagnosing why the AC will not start or keeps shutting off
  • Tracking down warm air issues and poor cooling
  • Fixing condensate drain clogs and water leaks
  • Replacing failed electrical components like capacitors or contactors
  • Repairing airflow issues tied to blower performance and restrictions
  • Checking refrigerant related symptoms and addressing the cause
  • Correcting thermostat or control problems

Why an AC runs but does not cool

This is a common La Jolla Mesa complaint. The system is on, you feel air, but the temperature does not drop the way it should.

  • Dirty air filter restricting airflow
  • Indoor coil issues that reduce heat transfer
  • Outdoor coil clogged with debris or salt air residue near the coast
  • Fan motor problems that reduce outdoor airflow
  • Low airflow from duct restrictions or blower issues
  • Thermostat placement or calibration problems
  • Refrigerant related symptoms that come from an underlying problem

What you can do right now is check the air filter and replace it if it is dirty, make sure supply vents are open and not blocked by furniture, confirm the thermostat is set to cool and the fan setting is correct, and look outside to see if the outdoor fan is spinning when the system runs. If the outdoor unit is running but the fan is not spinning, turn the system off and call for help.

Strange AC noises and what they can suggest

A little airflow noise is normal. Clanking, buzzing, grinding, or loud rattles are not. In La Jolla Mesa we often find outdoor units near patios, side yards, or walkway gates. A small rock, a loose fastener, or a panel that shifted can sound bigger than it is, but it is still smart to have it checked before a small issue turns into downtime.

  • Buzzing at start up can point to electrical components
  • Rattling outside can be loose panels or debris near the fan
  • Screeching can point to motor or blower issues
  • Clicking that repeats can be a control or electrical cycling issue
  • Hissing can suggest airflow or refrigerant related symptoms

Water leaking near the indoor unit

Water around the indoor equipment is usually a drainage problem. Your AC removes moisture from the air and that moisture has to drain correctly.

  • Clogged condensate drain line
  • Drain pan issues or misalignment
  • Indoor coil icing that melts later and overwhelms drainage
  • Improper slope or connection problems
  • Overflow safety switch shutting the system off

A condo closet air handler in La Jolla Mesa can drip into the hallway if the drain backs up. The first sign might be a faint musty smell or a little water line near the baseboard. If you see active leaking, turn cooling off to limit more condensate, mop up water to protect flooring, and do not keep resetting a tripped float switch without fixing the cause.

Why the system keeps turning on and off

Short cycling is when the system turns on, then shuts off, then repeats. It is hard on components and it rarely fixes itself. Short cycling can show up in La Jolla Mesa during coastal temperature swings, so we test the controls and system performance to find the reason.

  • Thermostat location issues or incorrect setup
  • Dirty filter or airflow restriction
  • Electrical component issues
  • Oversized equipment cycling quickly in mild weather
  • System controls reacting to a fault condition
  • Indoor coil issues
