Common cooling issues in Rolando

AC is running but not cooling

Common causes include low refrigerant from a leak, dirty coils, restricted airflow from a dirty filter or blower issue, debris on the outdoor coil, a failing capacitor or contactor, or thermostat and sensor problems. Home checks include confirming settings, changing a dirty filter, making sure vents are open, and looking outside to confirm the fan is spinning. Refrigerant handling and electrical testing should be left to a technician.

Weak airflow in older homes

Older duct layouts, additions, and converted spaces can create airflow trouble. Causes can include duct leaks in attic or crawl space, collapsed flex duct, dirty blower wheel and coil, closed or undersized returns, aging registers clogged with dust, or damper issues. If airflow is the root concern, Air Duct Maintenance in Rolando, CA can support long term performance after repairs.

Strange noises

Noises often point to a part or airflow issue that should be inspected. If you hear loud buzzing or grinding, shutting the system off can help avoid further damage until service is completed.

Noise What it can point to What you might notice
Buzzing Electrical issue, contactor, capacitor, loose wiring Unit tries to start then stops
Clicking Relay or control issue, thermostat signal problems Repeated attempts to turn on
Grinding Motor bearing trouble Noise worsens over time
Rattling Loose panel, debris, fan issue Gets louder when the unit ramps up
Hissing Possible refrigerant leak, airflow issue Cooling drops and runtime increases

Water leaks at the indoor unit

Common causes include a clogged condensate drain line, a cracked drain pan on older systems, a float switch shutting the system down after a backup, a frozen coil that later thaws into a puddle, or poor airflow that leads to icing. If you see water plus weak airflow, turning cooling off to let the coil thaw can help until service can address the root cause.

System will not turn on

Full shutdowns often involve electrical issues or safety lockouts, such as a tripped breaker, failed capacitor, bad contactor, thermostat power issues, a triggered condensate float switch, or motor failure. Basic checks include thermostat settings and breaker status. If it still will not start, a technician should handle it.
