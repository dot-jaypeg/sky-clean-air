Why is my AC turning on and off all the time

Short cycling is when the AC starts, stops, then starts again frequently. It wastes energy and can stress components.

  • Thermostat location or settings that cause rapid calls for cooling
  • Dirty coil causing the system to overheat and trip protection
  • Oversized equipment for the space
  • Low airflow, sometimes from a clogged filter or collapsed duct
  • Electrical issues like a weak capacitor or contactor
  • Drain safety switch tripping from a partially blocked condensate line

In Kensington, short cycling can also show up in homes with additions. A remodeled sunroom or converted garage can change the load on the system, and airflow may not match the new layout.

What does it mean when the AC is leaking water

A little moisture near the indoor unit is not something to ignore. Water can damage drywall, flooring, and nearby storage.

  • Condensate drain line blockage
  • Cracked drain pan
  • Frozen coil that thaws and overflows
  • Poor unit leveling
  • Loose or disconnected drain fittings
  • High indoor humidity combined with low airflow

If the indoor unit is in a closet, you may notice a damp smell first. If it is in a garage, you might spot a puddle near the wall. Either way, we check the drain path, confirm coil temperature, and look for airflow issues that can cause icing.

What you should check before calling

A few quick checks can save time and help you describe the problem clearly.

  • Confirm the thermostat is set to cool and the temperature is set lower than the room temperature
  • Replace the air filter if it is dirty or unknown
  • Check that supply vents are open and not blocked by furniture
  • Look for a tripped breaker if the system is completely dead
  • If the outdoor unit is running, listen for the fan and note any unusual noise
  • If you see ice on the indoor lines or coil, turn the system off and run the fan only

If you are unsure, stop after the filter step. No need to wrestle with panels. We can take it from there.
