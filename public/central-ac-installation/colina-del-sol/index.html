What types of central AC systems can you install

Central AC is not one single setup. The right choice depends on existing equipment, ductwork, layout, and how you use the space.

Typical options we install or coordinate

  • Split system central AC with an outdoor condenser and an indoor coil, usually paired with a furnace or air handler
  • Heat pump system that provides cooling and heating in a single system, often a good match for San Diego’s climate
  • AC with existing furnace that adds an evaporator coil and outdoor unit if the furnace and ductwork are a good match
  • Ducted air handler systems that can help when a home does not have a furnace but does have ducts or space to run them

If you are not sure what you have now, that is normal. We often hear I think it is a furnace but it might be a jet engine. We will identify the equipment, check labels, and confirm what can be reused.

How do you choose the right size system

System sizing is about matching capacity to the home’s real load, not just picking the biggest unit. Oversized systems can short cycle and leave humidity control and comfort uneven. Undersized systems can run longer and struggle on warmer days.

We base sizing on practical data points, including

  • Total conditioned square footage
  • Ceiling height and open floor plan areas
  • Window size, orientation, and shading
  • Insulation levels, especially attic insulation
  • Duct condition and airflow potential
  • Household heat sources like cooking, electronics, and occupancy patterns

A quick real world example from Colina del Sol. A home with the same square footage as the neighbor can need different equipment if one has a shaded yard and newer windows, while the other has west facing glass and a hot upstairs. Same size on paper, different comfort in real life.

Sometimes the ductwork is usable with minor improvements. Sometimes it needs targeted repairs. Either way, you will get a plain explanation. No mystery, no smoke signals.

How long does central AC installation take

Timing depends on what is already in place and what needs to be changed. A straightforward swap out with compatible ductwork is different from a first time install that needs ducts, electrical updates, and equipment relocation.

Typical factors that affect schedule

  • Whether ducts are already present and in workable shape
  • Attic access and how easy it is to route lines and drains
  • Electrical capacity and panel space
  • Permit requirements and inspection scheduling
  • Weather and site access, including driveway and gate access
  • Lead time for the specific equipment chosen

We will give you a realistic timeline based on your home. Not a vague guess. If something changes after we open up access panels or inspect duct runs, we will talk it through before moving forward.

What does the installation process look like

Most customers want the play by play. Fair. Here is what a central AC installation commonly involves.

  • Protect the work areas with careful access through hallways, closets, and attic openings
  • Set or replace indoor components such as the evaporator coil, air handler or furnace connections, and condensate drain routing
  • Set the outdoor condenser with stable mounting, airflow placement, and proper clearances
  • Run and connect refrigerant lines with neat, serviceable routing
  • Complete electrical connections and controls including disconnect, control wiring, and thermostat wiring as needed
  • Perform airflow checks and system start up to verify temperatures, airflow, and basic operation
  • Final walkthrough to show thermostat functions, filter location, and what to watch for

We keep communication simple. If you hear something like We are going to pull the lineset through the attic, we will also tell you what that means for your ceiling access and how long that portion usually takes.

Will my thermostat need to be replaced

Sometimes yes, sometimes no. Older thermostats may not support modern features or may not be compatible with certain system types.

We will check

  • Wiring compatibility
  • Placement and whether it is in a heat prone spot
  • User preferences like simple controls vs scheduling features
  • Whether you want a thermostat that can manage a heat pump properly

If your thermostat is in a hallway that turns into a warm tunnel every afternoon, it may be telling the AC the wrong story. A small change in placement can improve comfort.

What can I do to help my new central AC perform well

Central AC works best when the home supports airflow and heat control. You do not need to remodel your house, but a few practical steps can help.

Helpful prep and habits

  • Replace or clean filters on a regular schedule
  • Keep supply vents open, even in rooms you rarely use
  • Make sure return grilles are not blocked by furniture
  • Use window coverings on west facing windows during late afternoons
  • Seal obvious gaps around doors if you feel warm drafts
  • Keep the outdoor unit area free of stored items and overgrowth

A quick note on closing vents. It is tempting to close vents to push air elsewhere. Many duct systems do not respond well to that. If a room is too cold or too warm, it is better to look at balancing, duct conditions, and register placement.

What are common comfort complaints in Colina del Sol, and how do installs address them

Possible contributors

  • Mounting and placement issues

  • High static pressure from restrictive ducts

The living room is fine, the kitchen is not

Possible contributors

  • Heat from cooking and appliances
  • Missing supply coverage
  • Layout that traps warm air

Sometimes it is as simple as improving airflow distribution to a high heat area.
