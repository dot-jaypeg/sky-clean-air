What is included in Air Duct Installation

Most people think ductwork is just metal tubes hidden in a ceiling. In real life, it is a full system. Each piece affects airflow, comfort, and how hard your HVAC equipment has to work.

A typical duct installation may include

  • Reviewing your current HVAC setup and where ducts can physically run
  • Planning supply and return duct locations
  • Removing damaged or poorly routed duct sections when needed
  • Installing new duct runs in attic, crawl space, walls, or soffits
  • Connecting to plenums, boots, registers, and returns
  • Sealing joints and transitions to reduce air loss
  • Supporting ducts correctly so they do not sag over time
  • Checking airflow balance room to room

Should you repair existing ducts or install new ones

Sometimes a repair is enough. Sometimes it is not. The best answer comes from what we see on site.

Repair might make sense when

  • One or two sections are disconnected
  • A small area is torn or crushed
  • A single run is poorly supported and sagging
  • Sealing and minor rework would restore airflow

New installation or replacement might make sense when

  • Duct sizes are mismatched across the system
  • Runs are too long with too many sharp turns
  • Materials are deteriorating across multiple sections
  • Duct layout no longer fits a remodel or room use
  • Returns are missing or placed poorly for good circulation

If your home has had a few additions over the years, ductwork can look like a plate of spaghetti. At that point, clean routing and correct sizing can do more than another patch. What types of ductwork can be installed

Different spaces call for different materials and build approaches. We install ductwork that fits the structure, access, and HVAC setup.

Common duct types

  • Sheet metal ducts Often used for main trunks and durability, with clean transitions.
  • Flexible duct Useful for shorter runs to registers when installed with proper support and gentle bends.
  • Duct board Sometimes used for plenums or trunks in certain layouts.

What matters most is not just the material. It is how it is routed, supported, and sealed. A good duct path beats a fancy looking duct path every time.
