Common duct types used in installation

Duct installation is not a single material choice. The right option depends on access, shape constraints, and performance goals.

  • Sheet metal ducts often used for main trunk lines and durability in straight runs
  • Flexible duct useful for branch runs to ceiling registers where space is tight and routing must be supported to avoid sagging
  • Duct board sometimes used where built in insulation is helpful and protection and clean cuts matter

Returns and why they matter

Supply ducts push air in and returns pull air back. When return air is limited, the system can struggle even with plenty of supply air.

  • Whistling at doors when the system runs
  • Rooms that feel stuffy with the door closed
  • A strong pressure difference between rooms
  • Dust patterns around door gaps or registers

Return placement can be important in homes where remodels created larger open areas. Return airflow helps the system keep up without turning the fan into a wind tunnel. Some homes also combine duct updates with AC Cleaning in Lower Hermosa, CA to address buildup in the air path.

Routing in tight attics and crawl spaces

Lower Hermosa homes often have attic spaces with low clearance and obstacles from past upgrades. We focus on direct runs, proper support, avoiding tight bends, maintaining clearance from heat sources, and planning service access for future HVAC work.

  • Keeping runs as direct as possible
  • Supporting ducts so they do not sag
  • Avoiding tight bends that restrict airflow
  • Maintaining clearance from recessed lights and other heat sources
  • Keeping access workable for future service

If you have a crawl space, we also pay attention to moisture conditions, hanging and strapping so ducts stay off the ground, and sealing connections so air does not leak into the crawl space.

How long installation can take

Timing depends on scope, access, and how much of the system is being installed or replaced. Factors include attic height, number of rooms and runs, removal of old ducts, routing challenges around framing or wiring, ceiling access needs for boots or returns, and weather conditions that affect attic work comfort and safety.

Keeping the system quieter and the air cleaner

Duct installation can help if noise is related to airflow issues, vibration, or poorly supported ductwork. We pay attention to securing ducts to reduce rattles, avoiding tight turns near registers that create whooshing, keeping flex duct routed and supported properly, and choosing sensible register sizes when changes are being made.

Duct installation is construction work, so keeping things tidy matters. We use containment and careful handling to reduce debris movement through the home. Practical steps include covering registers during dusty phases, vacuuming loose debris around work areas, sealing connections so attic dust is less likely to enter the air stream, and checking filter placement.
