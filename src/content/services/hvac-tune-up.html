What we actually do on a tune-up

Every system is a little different, so the exact steps depend on whether you have an air conditioner with a furnace, a heat pump, or a packaged unit. A typical visit covers:

Outside

  • Inspect the condenser or heat pump and clear debris from around it
  • Check the coil for dirt, bent fins and corrosion
  • Test the capacitor, contactor and electrical connections
  • Listen to the fan motor and compressor start-up

Inside

  • Check the air filter, and tell you if it's the wrong size or doesn't fit well
  • Look over the blower, indoor coil area and wiring
  • Check the condensate drain line and pan
  • On furnaces, look at the burners, ignition and safety controls

The whole system

  • Run it in cooling and heating and check temperatures at the vents
  • Check the thermostat's settings and operation
  • Take a look at accessible ductwork and registers for obvious leaks or disconnected runs

Afterward, the tech tells you what they found in plain terms. If something needs attention beyond the tune-up, you'll get a written price first. Nothing extra gets done without your okay.

Tune-up, maintenance or inspection?

These get used interchangeably, so here's how we think about them:

  • Tune-up: a yearly check and cleaning to keep a working system running well. That's this page.
  • Maintenance: ongoing care over time, including regular visits and follow-up on anything the tune-up turned up.
  • Inspection: a closer evaluation, useful when you're buying or selling a home, or you want a clear read on an older system's condition.

Why San Diego and OC systems benefit

People sometimes assume a mild climate means you can skip maintenance. Our systems deal with a few things that make yearly care worth it:

  • Santa Ana winds and fire season. Dust, ash and smoke load up filters and coils quickly. After a smoky week, it's common for a filter to look like it's been in for months.
  • Salt air on the coast. In Pacific Beach, Point Loma, Carlsbad or San Clemente, outdoor coils and electrical parts corrode faster. Catching that early matters.
  • Long inland run times. In El Cajon, Santee, Poway or Mission Viejo, the AC may run most of the afternoon for weeks. That's hard on capacitors and motors.
  • Hot attics. Many homes, from older Clairemont houses to 1980s tracts in Irvine and Lake Forest, have the air handler in the attic. Drain problems up there can end up in your ceiling.
  • Pets and remodel dust. Pet hair and drywall dust clog filters and coat blower wheels faster than most people expect.

When to schedule

Spring is ideal for cooling, before the first real heat wave, and fall is a good time to check the heating side before cool nights set in. If you're only doing one visit a year, schedule it before summer. Appointments fill up once the weather turns hot, so booking early helps.

Your part between visits

A tune-up once a year does a lot, but a few simple habits make it go further:

  • Check the filter every month or two, and right after Santa Ana winds or a smoky stretch. Replace it when it looks gray.
  • Keep two feet of clear space around the outdoor unit. Trim back shrubs and don't stack things against it.
  • Rinse the outdoor coil gently with a garden hose a couple of times a year if you live near the coast. Low pressure only, and with the power off.
  • Keep supply vents and returns open and unblocked by furniture or rugs. Closing vents in unused rooms usually does more harm than good.
  • Pay attention to changes. A new noise, a musty smell at start-up or a room that suddenly runs warm is worth a call before it becomes a repair.

What a tune-up can't do

A tune-up won't turn a 20-year-old system into a new one, and it won't fix ducts that were installed poorly. What it will do is keep a healthy system efficient, flag parts that are on their way out, and give you an honest sense of how much life your system has left. If we see that your system is getting close to the end, around the 15-year mark or showing serious wear, we'll tell you so you can plan instead of scrambling. That's not a sales pitch. Plenty of older systems just need regular care.
