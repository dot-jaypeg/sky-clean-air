The week your old AC can't keep up

Most people start thinking about a new air conditioner on the worst possible day. It's September, it's 101 in Escondido, the old unit has been running since noon, and the upstairs bedrooms are still 85. Or it's the first Santa Ana week in Mission Viejo, and the system that limped through August finally gives up.

If that's where you are, we can help quickly. If you're planning ahead, even better. Either way, the goal is the same: a system that handles the hottest week of the year without drama, and doesn't cost a fortune to run the rest of it.

Who usually needs a new AC

  • Your current system is 15 or more years old and the repairs are getting more frequent or more expensive.
  • It runs on R-22, the older refrigerant that has been phased out. Servicing those systems gets harder and pricier every year.
  • You've never had central air. Plenty of homes in Clairemont, Point Loma and older parts of Irvine were built with a furnace and nothing else, because the coast didn't need it. Summers have changed a lot of minds.
  • The house has changed. A room addition, a finished garage or a remodel can leave the original system undersized.
  • It never really kept up. Some systems were the wrong size from day one.

An older system isn't automatically a lost cause. If a repair makes sense, we'll say so. See AC repair if you're not sure yet.

Sizing: the part that decides whether you'll be happy

The most common mistake we see in new installs is simply replacing a 3-ton unit with another 3-ton unit because that's what was there. That old unit may have been oversized, undersized, or sized for a house that didn't have new windows and attic insulation.

An oversized system cools the air fast and shuts off, over and over. The house feels clammy, the equipment wears out sooner, and some rooms never catch up. An undersized one runs all afternoon on a hot day and still loses. Neither is what you paid for.

So before we recommend anything, we look at the actual house:

  • Square footage, ceiling heights and how many stories
  • Which way the house faces and how much afternoon sun hits it
  • Window size and type
  • Attic insulation, and whether ducts run through a hot attic
  • Where you live, because a home in Santee needs different capacity than the same floor plan in Encinitas

Your ducts matter as much as the new unit

A new air conditioner connected to leaky, crushed or undersized ducts will underperform, sometimes badly. In a lot of 1970s through 1990s tract homes across south Orange County and inland San Diego, the original flex duct in the attic has sagged, separated at the joints, or been stepped on during other work.

Duct work is where Sky Clean Air started, so we pay close attention to it. During your estimate we'll look at the ductwork and let you know whether it's fine as-is, needs sealing or repairs, or should be replaced. If the ducts are in good shape, we'll tell you that too.

Install day, start to finish

  1. Protecting your home. We lay down floor protection along the path to the attic or closet and keep the work area contained.
  2. Removing the old equipment. The old condenser and indoor coil come out, and refrigerant is recovered by EPA-certified techs rather than vented.
  3. Setting the new system. The outdoor unit goes on a level pad, the indoor coil or air handler is installed, and refrigerant lines, electrical and the condensate drain are connected.
  4. Connecting the ducts. Transitions are sealed so the air you're paying to cool actually reaches your rooms.
  5. Thermostat. We connect your thermostat or install a new one if you'd like an upgrade.
  6. Testing. We charge and verify the system, check temperatures at the registers, and make sure the drain is flowing.
  7. Walkthrough. We show you how to use it, where the filter goes, and how often to change it.

Most straightforward replacements are done in a day. New installs in homes without existing AC, or jobs that include duct replacement, take longer, and we'll give you a realistic timeline up front.

Picking equipment without the sales pitch

You'll hear a lot about efficiency ratings and variable-speed compressors. They matter, but how much depends on where you live. In hotter inland areas like El Cajon, Poway or Lake Forest, where the AC runs for months, higher-efficiency equipment tends to pay you back on your electric bill. Closer to the coast, where it runs a few weeks a year, a solid mid-range system is often the smarter spend.

We'll usually lay out a couple of options at different price points and tell you which one we'd pick for your house and why. If a heat pump would do the job of both your AC and furnace, we'll mention that as well.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does AC installation take?

A standard replacement where ducts and wiring are already in place is usually finished in one day. A first-time install or a job with new ductwork can take longer, and we'll give you a timeline with your estimate.

Do I need new ductwork?

Not always. We inspect the existing ducts during the estimate. If they're sealed, sized reasonably and in decent shape, we'll reuse them. If they're leaking badly or collapsed, replacing them now is usually cheaper than doing it separately later.

Can I add central air to a house that only has a furnace?

Often, yes. If the furnace and ducts are in good condition, we can add an indoor coil and an outdoor condenser. If the furnace is also old, a heat pump or a full system replacement may be the better value.

What size AC do I need?

It depends on the house, not just the square footage. Sun exposure, windows, insulation, duct location and your microclimate all play a part. We'll work it out during the estimate rather than guess.

Will you take the old unit away?

Yes. We remove the old equipment, and our EPA-certified techs recover the refrigerant properly.

Would a mini-split be better for my home?

If you don't have ducts, or you only need to cool part of the house, it might be. Take a look at our mini-split installation page, or ask us to price both options.
