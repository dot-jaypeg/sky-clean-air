Let's start with what UV won't do

If you've shopped for UV air purifiers online, you've probably seen big promises: kills 99.9% of germs, wipes out viruses, hospital-grade air. We don't make those claims, and we'd be careful of anyone who does.

Here's the honest version. Air moves through your HVAC system quickly, so any single germ floating past a UV lamp gets only a brief exposure. A UV light isn't a filter, either. It doesn't catch dust, pollen, pet dander or wildfire smoke. Those are particles, and particles are a filtration job.

So why would anyone install one? Because there's one place UV does its best work, and it's a place that causes real problems in Southern California homes.

The coil and the drain pan

When your air conditioner runs, the indoor coil gets cold and pulls moisture out of the air. That water drips into a drain pan and runs out a condensate line. It's supposed to happen. But it means the coil and pan are dark, damp and constantly fed with dust, which is exactly where mold and bacteria like to grow.

Over time, that growth can form a slimy film on the coil. You might notice it as:

  • A musty, "dirty sock" smell for the first few minutes after the AC kicks on
  • A drain line that clogs with sludge and backs up
  • Airflow that seems weaker than it used to be
  • A coil that looks dark or slick when a tech pulls the panel

A UV-C lamp mounted so it shines directly on the coil and drain pan runs around the clock, and that constant exposure is what makes it effective there. It helps keep mold and bacteria from building up on those surfaces in the first place. The coil stays cleaner, the smell tends to stay away, and the drain is less likely to clog.

Why this matters more near the coast

If you live in Ocean Beach, Pacific Beach, Encinitas, Carlsbad, Oceanside, or up the Orange County coast in San Clemente, Dana Point or Huntington Beach, you know the marine layer. Mornings are gray and damp for weeks at a time, especially in May and June. That extra humidity keeps coils and pans wet longer, and homes near the water are where we see musty AC smells most often.

Inland homes aren't immune. A system in Santee or Mission Viejo that runs hard all summer produces plenty of condensation too. But coastal homes are where a coil-focused UV light earns its keep most clearly.

Coil-mounted vs. air-stream lamps

There are two basic ways UV gets installed:

  • Coil-facing lamps are aimed at the coil and drain pan. This is the setup we recommend most, since it's aimed at the problem UV handles best.
  • Air-stream lamps are placed in the ductwork to expose moving air. They may reduce some microorganisms in the air, but because air passes by so quickly, the effect is limited compared to what you'd get from constant exposure on a surface. We'll tell you plainly if we don't think one is worth it for your home.

We also only install lamps that are designed not to produce ozone. Some UV bulbs emit a wavelength that creates ozone, which you don't want circulating through your house.

A good fit, a poor fit

UV makes sense if you've had musty smells from the AC, recurring drain clogs, a coil that's been cleaned for biological growth before, or a humid coastal home where the system runs a lot.

UV is probably not your answer if your main issue is dust, allergies, pet dander or smoke. For those, a whole-home air purifier or media filter will do far more. Many homes end up with both: a good filter for the air, and a UV lamp for the coil.

And if your coil is already coated, a UV lamp won't strip it clean overnight. In that case, we'll usually recommend an AC cleaning first, then UV to help keep it that way.

What the installation involves

  1. Inspect the system. We look at the air handler, coil, drain pan and condensate line, and check how much room there is to mount a lamp with a clear view of the coil.
  2. Clean if needed. If there's existing growth, we'll talk about cleaning before installing, so the lamp starts with a clean surface.
  3. Mount the lamp. The fixture goes through a small opening in the cabinet or plenum near the coil, with the bulb positioned to cover as much of the coil and pan as possible.
  4. Wire and safety. The lamp is wired to power, and where the equipment supports it, set up so it shuts off when the panel is opened. UV-C can hurt your eyes and skin, so you should never look directly at a lit lamp.
  5. Walk you through it. You'll know where the lamp is, how to tell it's on, and when the bulb is due for replacement.

Most installs are done in an hour or two.

Upkeep: the bulb matters

UV bulbs don't burn out the way a lightbulb does. They keep glowing long after their UV output has dropped, so a lamp can look fine while doing very little. Most need replacing roughly once a year, depending on the model. We'll note the date on your system, and bulb swaps are easy to handle during your regular HVAC maintenance or as a separate UV bulb replacement.

Keeping the bulb current is the difference between a UV light that works and one that's just decoration.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a UV air purifier kill viruses?

UV-C light can damage viruses and bacteria with enough exposure, but air moves through your system too fast for a lamp to treat it thoroughly. We don't sell UV as protection against illness. Its real strength is keeping the coil and drain pan free of mold and bacterial growth.

Will a UV light help with my allergies?

Probably not much. Most allergy triggers, like pollen, dust and pet dander, are particles, and UV doesn't capture particles. Better filtration is the upgrade that helps allergies.

Will it get rid of the musty smell from my AC?

If the smell comes from growth on the coil or in the drain pan, which it often does, UV can help keep it from coming back, especially after the coil has been cleaned. If the odor comes from somewhere else, like the ductwork or a moisture problem in the house, we'll help track that down.

How often does the UV bulb need replacing?

Most bulbs need replacing about once a year, depending on the model. They keep glowing after their output drops, so don't go by whether it looks lit.

Is UV light safe to have in my HVAC system?

Yes, when it's installed correctly inside the equipment where no one can see it. The lamp shouldn't be looked at directly, which is why it's mounted inside the cabinet. We install ozone-free lamps.
