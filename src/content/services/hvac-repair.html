Tell us what it's doing

The fastest repairs start with a good description. When you call, we'll ask a few questions. Is air coming out of the vents at all? Is it warm or cool? Does the outdoor unit hum, click or stay silent? When did you last change the filter? Your answers help us send a tech with the right parts on the truck, so the fix is more likely to happen on the first visit.

Here are some of the symptoms we hear most, and what's often behind them.

Common problems and likely causes

The AC runs, but the air isn't cold

Often a dirty or iced-over coil, a refrigerant leak, a failed capacitor that's keeping the outdoor fan or compressor from starting, or simply a filter so clogged that air can barely get through. Near the coast in Ocean Beach or Pacific Beach, corroded outdoor coils and electrical contacts are a regular culprit.

The system turns on and off every few minutes

Short cycling can come from an overheating unit, a dirty coil, a thermostat in a bad spot, a refrigerant problem, or equipment that's too big for the house. Left alone, it wears parts out quickly.

Some rooms are comfortable, others aren't

Uneven temperatures are frequently a duct issue rather than an equipment issue. In older homes around Clairemont, North Park and Point Loma, we regularly find disconnected or crushed ducts in the attic dumping cool air where nobody lives. Airflow balancing or duct repair can make a big difference.

Water around the indoor unit

Usually a clogged condensate drain or a cracked drain pan. It's an easy fix if you catch it early, and an expensive ceiling repair if you don't, especially when the air handler is in the attic.

The furnace won't light, or smells like burning

A dusty smell the first time you turn on the heat in fall is normal and should clear quickly. A persistent burning or electrical smell isn't. Turn the system off and call us. Ignition problems are often a dirty flame sensor or a failed igniter. See furnace repair for more.

New noises

Squealing, grinding, banging or rattling usually points to a motor, a fan blade, a loose panel or something that's worked its way into the unit. Shut it off before a small problem becomes a big one.

A few things to check before you call

These won't fix a real failure, but they sometimes save you a service call:

  • Make sure the thermostat is set to heat or cool, and that its batteries aren't dead.
  • Check the breaker for the indoor and outdoor units.
  • Look at the air filter. If it's gray and matted, replace it. After Santa Ana winds or a smoky week, filters load up fast.
  • Make sure the outdoor unit isn't buried in leaves, lawn clippings or overgrown plants.

If a breaker trips again after you reset it, leave it off and call. Don't keep resetting it.

How a repair visit works

  1. Diagnosis. The tech checks the whole system, not just the part you mentioned: electrical components, refrigerant pressures, airflow, the drain line, and the thermostat.
  2. Explanation. You'll hear what's wrong in plain language, and see it when we can show you. No jargon dump.
  3. Written price. Before we do any work, you get a written, upfront price for the repair. You decide whether to go ahead.
  4. Repair and test. We fix it, then run the system and confirm it's heating or cooling the way it should.

If the system is older and the repair is a big one, the tech will say so. Sometimes the honest answer is that a major repair on a worn-out system isn't a good use of your money. When that's the case, we'll lay out the numbers for replacement too and let you choose. Most of the time, though, a repair is exactly what you need.

Repairs we make all the time

Most HVAC repairs fall into a handful of categories. Knowing them won't make you a technician, but it helps the conversation:

  • Electrical parts: capacitors, contactors, relays and wiring that have worn out, burned or corroded
  • Motors and fans: blower motors, condenser fan motors and the fan blades themselves
  • Refrigerant issues: finding leaks, repairing them where it makes sense, and restoring the correct charge
  • Drain problems: clogged condensate lines, failed pumps and cracked pans
  • Heating components: igniters, flame sensors and safety switches on furnaces
  • Controls: thermostats that have failed, are wired wrong or sit in a spot that gives them bad readings

If the problem turns out to be a duct or airflow issue instead, we'll tell you that too, rather than replacing parts that were never the cause.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an HVAC repair cost?

It depends entirely on what's wrong. A failed capacitor and a failed compressor are very different jobs. Our tech will diagnose the problem and give you a written price before any repair work starts.

Can you come out today?

Often, yes. Same-day and next-day appointments are frequently available, but it depends on the schedule and your location. Call and we'll give you the soonest opening.

Is it safe to keep running my system while I wait?

If it's making grinding or banging noises, smells like burning, is leaking water, or keeps tripping a breaker, turn it off. If it's just not cooling or heating as well as usual, it's generally fine to run it until we arrive, but a fresh filter is a good idea.

Do you repair heat pumps and ductless systems?

Yes. We repair central air conditioners, furnaces, heat pumps and ductless mini-splits.

How do I avoid the next repair?

Change your filter regularly and schedule a yearly HVAC tune-up. Many of the repairs we make, like clogged drains, dirty coils and weak capacitors, are the kind of thing a tune-up catches early.
