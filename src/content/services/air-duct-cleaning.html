The first job we ever did

Sky Clean Air started in 2018 with one rented van and a set of duct-cleaning equipment. We've added a lot of services since then, but duct cleaning is still the one we do more than anything else, and it's how most San Diego and Orange County families first get to know us.

Here's the short version of what it is. Your system pulls air from the house through the return, runs it past the filter and the coil, and pushes it back out through the supply ducts to every room. Over the years, the stuff the filter doesn't catch settles along the inside of those ducts: household dust, pet hair and dander, pollen, drywall dust from that kitchen remodel. Every time the system runs, some of it breaks loose and rides back into your living room. Duct cleaning takes it out at the source.

What you might be noticing

Most people call us because of one of these:

  • A puff of dust from the vents when the AC or heat first comes on
  • Gray fuzz or dark streaks around the ceiling registers
  • Furniture that needs dusting again two days after you did it
  • A stale or dusty smell while the system runs
  • Allergies that act up more inside the house than outside
  • A recent remodel, floor sanding or drywall work
  • You just bought the house and have no idea when the ducts were last cleaned

If you've been changing the filter faithfully and the house still feels dusty, the ducts are often the missing piece.

What duct cleaning will and won't do

We'd rather be straight with you up front. Clean ducts mean less dust circulating through the house, fewer odors riding along with the airflow, and a system that isn't pushing air through a layer of debris. People with pets and allergies usually notice the difference.

What it won't do is fix a leaky or crushed duct. If one bedroom has always been hot, or your bills keep climbing, that's usually a physical problem in the attic, and it calls for air duct repair or sealing instead. It also won't remove a strong musty or smoke odor on its own; that's where duct sanitizing comes in. If we see either of those issues during your cleaning, we'll show you and let you decide.

How the visit goes

  1. Walk-through and price. Your tech counts the vents, finds the return and the air handler, checks attic access and looks for anything unusual. You get a written, upfront price before any work begins.
  2. Protecting the house. Shoe covers go on. Drop cloths go down where we'll be working, and furniture or electronics near vents get covered.
  3. Negative pressure. We connect a high-powered vacuum with HEPA filtration to the system. That puts the ducts under suction, so debris gets pulled into the machine instead of drifting into your rooms.
  4. Agitation, run by run. With the vacuum running, we work each supply line with rotating brushes and air whips to knock loose what's stuck to the duct walls.
  5. Returns and trunk line. The return side pulls in the most dust, so it gets extra time, along with the main trunk.
  6. Registers and grilles. Each one comes off, gets cleaned and goes back on.
  7. Final check. We confirm airflow at the vents, show you before-and-after photos and clean up. We'll also give you a straight answer on filters: which size, how often, and whether an upgrade is worth it.

Most single-family homes take a few hours. Size, number of vents and attic access all play into it.

Why San Diego and OC ducts fill up

A few things about living here speed up buildup.

  • Beach towns. In Pacific Beach, Ocean Beach, Coronado, Huntington Beach and San Clemente, fine sand and salt come in on shoes, towels and dogs. The marine layer adds moisture, and dust sticks better to damp surfaces.
  • Santa Ana winds and fire season. Dry east winds carry dust across the county, and smoke particles are small enough to settle deep in the ductwork. If your house still smells faintly of smoke after a bad fire week, that's often why.
  • Older homes. Many of the 1950s–70s houses in Clairemont, North Park and Point Loma, and the 1970s–90s tract homes in Irvine, Mission Viejo and Lake Forest, still have their original ducts. Some have never been cleaned.
  • Remodels. Drywall and sanding dust is extremely fine. If the system ran during construction, a lot of it is in your ducts now.
  • Hot inland valleys. Homes in El Cajon, Santee, Escondido and Poway run the AC hard for months, which moves more air, and more dust, through the system.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I have my ducts cleaned?

For most homes, every few years is plenty. Sooner makes sense if you have pets, someone in the house has allergies, you've just finished a remodel, or you moved into a home with an unknown history.

How long does it take?

Most single-family homes take a few hours. Larger homes, two-story systems or tight attic access can take longer. Your tech will give you a time estimate during the walk-through.

Can I stay home during the cleaning?

Yes. Most people do. We'll need access to the vents in each room and to the air handler, and it does get a little noisy while the vacuum is running.

Will duct cleaning help my allergies?

It can help by removing the dust, dander and pollen that has settled in the ducts, so less of it gets blown around the house. It's not a medical treatment, though, and it works best alongside a good filter changed on schedule.

Will cleaning lower my energy bill?

Clean ducts help air move freely, but if your bills are high, leaks in the ductwork are a more likely cause. If we see signs of leaking during your cleaning, we'll point it out.
