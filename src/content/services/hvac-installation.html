The biggest mistake in HVAC installation happens before anyone picks up a tool

It's sizing. A lot of new systems in San Diego get chosen by reading the label on the old unit and ordering the same tonnage, or by going one size bigger "to be safe." It sounds reasonable. It's often wrong.

An oversized air conditioner cools the air fast, shuts off, then kicks back on a few minutes later. That short cycling wears out parts, leaves rooms feeling clammy on humid coastal days, and makes the house swing between too cold and too warm. An undersized one runs all afternoon in August and still loses ground in the upstairs bedrooms. Either way you paid for a new system and you're still uncomfortable.

So before we recommend any equipment, we work out what your home actually needs.

What a load calculation looks at

A load calculation is a room-by-room estimate of how much heat your home gains in summer and loses in winter. It's the basis for the equipment size we recommend. On the estimate visit, our tech gathers the details that drive it:

  • Square footage and layout, including ceiling heights, stories, and open versus closed floor plans
  • Windows: how many, how big, which way they face, and whether they're older single-pane glass
  • Insulation and attic conditions, since a thin or damaged attic layer changes the numbers a lot
  • Where you live. A house in Pacific Beach with a marine layer most mornings has a very different summer load than the same floor plan in Escondido, Poway or Mission Viejo
  • How you use the house: home offices, a room over the garage that always runs hot, or plans for an addition

Things you've changed since the last system went in matter too. New windows, a remodel, or fresh attic insulation can mean your home needs less capacity than it used to.

Choosing the equipment

Once we know the load, we talk through the kinds of systems that make sense for your home. We'll lay out the tradeoffs in plain terms rather than steering you toward the priciest box on the truck.

Heat pumps

A heat pump heats and cools with one outdoor unit. Our winters are mild, so heat pumps suit a lot of San Diego and Orange County homes well, and they're a natural fit if you're thinking about moving away from gas. More on that on our heat pump installation page.

Air conditioner with a gas furnace

The classic split system. It still makes sense for plenty of homes, especially where a gas line and furnace location are already in place and working well.

Ductless mini-splits

For homes without ducts, older homes where running ductwork would mean tearing up ceilings, or a single addition or garage conversion, a mini-split system can be the cleaner answer.

Single-stage or variable-speed

Single-stage equipment runs at full blast or not at all. Variable-speed systems ramp up and down, which usually means quieter operation and steadier temperatures. They cost more up front. Whether that's worth it depends on your house and how long you plan to stay, and we'll tell you honestly which way we'd lean.

We look at the whole home, not just the unit

A new system connected to leaky, crushed or undersized ducts won't perform the way it should. That's one reason we look at the whole house during an installation estimate, not just the equipment location.

If we find something that will hold back your new system, we'll show you and explain the options, such as duct sealing or repairs. If the ducts are in good shape, we'll say so and leave them alone.

How installation day goes

  1. Protecting your home. We cover floors and the path between the door and the work area.
  2. Removing the old equipment. The old unit comes out and we prepare the pad, closet or attic platform for the new one.
  3. Setting and connecting the new system. Indoor and outdoor units, refrigerant lines, drain line, electrical and thermostat.
  4. Start-up and testing. We check refrigerant charge, airflow and temperatures, and confirm the system is running the way it was designed to.
  5. Walkthrough. We show you the thermostat, the filter location and what maintenance looks like going forward.

Many standard replacements wrap up in a day or two. Jobs that add ductwork or change the system type can take longer, and we'll give you a realistic timeline with your estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can't you just install the same size unit I have now?

We can, but we'd rather confirm it's right first. Plenty of older systems were oversized to begin with, and homes change over the years with new windows, insulation or additions. A load calculation tells us what your home needs today.

How long does an HVAC installation take?

A straightforward swap is often done in a day or two. Adding ducts, changing system types or moving equipment to a new location takes longer. You'll get a timeline with your estimate.

Is a heat pump a good idea in San Diego?

For many homes, yes. Mild winters play to a heat pump's strengths. It isn't the right call for every house, though, and we'll explain why if we think another setup fits better.

Do I need new ductwork with a new system?

Not always. We inspect your ducts during the estimate. If they're sound, we reuse them. If they're leaking, crushed or undersized, we'll show you what we found and what fixing it would involve.
