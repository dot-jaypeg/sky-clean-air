Your AC is probably working in the hottest spot on the property

Here's something most homeowners don't think about. In a large share of San Diego and Orange County homes, especially single-story ranches and tract homes without basements, the air handler and most of the ductwork live in the attic. On a summer afternoon in Santee, Escondido or Lake Forest, that attic can get far hotter than it is outside.

So your air conditioner makes cold air, then pushes it through ducts surrounded by baking air before it ever reaches a vent. Any heat that gets through the ceiling below adds to the load. Good attic insulation won't cool the attic itself, but it slows the heat moving down into your rooms, and it's a big part of why some houses stay comfortable at 4 p.m. while others don't.

Replacement means taking out insulation that's no longer doing its job and putting in new material at the right depth, with the gaps sealed first.

When replacement is worth it

  • Rooms directly under the attic are noticeably hotter in summer and cooler on winter mornings than the rest of the house.
  • Your AC runs through most of the afternoon on warm days and still barely keeps up.
  • The insulation you can see from the hatch is thin, gray, flattened or patchy, with joists showing through.
  • There's been rodent activity, a roof leak, or smoke and ash exposure.
  • You've had a remodel, re-roof, or solar install, and the insulation got trampled or pushed around.

If your insulation is clean and dry but just shallow, you may be able to add more on top instead of starting over. If it's contaminated, it needs to come out first. See our page on attic insulation removal for how that works.

Blown-in vs. batt: which one fits your attic

These are the two most common options for attic floors, and neither is universally better. It comes down to the shape of your attic and what's in it.

Blown-in insulation

Loose fiberglass or cellulose is blown through a hose and settles into an even blanket across the attic floor. It fills around wiring, pipes, framing and odd corners without leaving the small gaps that let heat through. It's also the practical choice for low, cramped attics that are hard to crawl through, which describes a lot of 1950s and 60s homes around Clairemont and La Mesa. Most full attic replacements we do in older SoCal homes end up being blown-in. Learn more about blown-in insulation.

Batt insulation

Batts are pre-cut blankets, usually fiberglass, laid between the joists. They work well in open attics with standard joist spacing and fewer obstructions, and they're easier to lift and move later if you need access to wiring or ducts. The catch is fit. Batts have to be cut carefully around every pipe, wire and fixture. Sloppy batt work leaves gaps, and gaps add up. See batt and roll insulation for more.

What about spray foam or radiant barrier?

Those are separate options with their own trade-offs, and they're better suited to specific situations. If you're curious, ask during the estimate and we'll tell you honestly whether either makes sense for your house.

How much insulation is enough?

Insulation is rated by R-value, which measures how well it resists heat moving through it. Higher is better, and for attics in our area, R-38 is a common target. What that means in inches depends on the material. The practical point: many older homes in San Diego County have a fraction of that, sometimes only a few inches of settled material.

We'll measure what you have now and recommend a depth that makes sense for your attic, your budget and how hot your area gets. Coastal homes in Carlsbad or Dana Point and inland homes in El Cajon or Mission Viejo don't face the same summer heat, and the recommendation should reflect that.

The replacement, start to finish

  1. Look first. A tech checks the insulation's depth and condition, the attic's ventilation, and the ducts and equipment up there. You get photos and a written price.
  2. Remove what needs to go. If the old insulation is contaminated or badly compressed, we vacuum it out and clean the attic floor.
  3. Seal before you insulate. With the floor bare, we close gaps around plumbing stacks, wiring holes and the hatch. Insulation slows heat, but it doesn't stop air from leaking through. Air sealing handles that part.
  4. Keep the vents open. We make sure eave vents aren't buried, so the attic can still breathe.
  5. Install to depth. New insulation goes in evenly across the attic, with care taken around recessed lights and other fixtures that need clearance.
  6. Walk through it. We show you the finished attic, and point out anything else we noticed along the way.

The ducts are part of this conversation

Since we're an HVAC company, we're not only looking at the insulation. Attic ductwork in older homes is often torn, crushed, poorly connected or thinly insulated itself. There's not much sense in insulating the attic floor beautifully while a leaky duct next to it dumps cool air into the attic. If we see duct problems, we'll show you, and duct repair or sealing can often be done while we're already up there.

We'll also give you an honest read on your AC or furnace. If it's more than about 15 years old and showing its age, we'll tell you. If it's fine, we'll tell you that too. New insulation doesn't mean you need new equipment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is blown-in or batt insulation better for my attic?

For most older San Diego and Orange County attics, especially low or cluttered ones, blown-in gives more complete coverage. Batts can be a good fit for open attics with standard framing. We'll recommend one after seeing your attic, and explain why.

Do I have to remove my old insulation first?

Not always. If it's clean and dry, new insulation can often go on top. If it has rodent waste, water damage or mold, or it's heavily compressed, it should come out first.

How long does attic insulation replacement take?

Adding insulation to a clean attic can often be done in a day. When removal and air sealing are part of the job, plan on one to two days for most homes.

Will new insulation make my house cooler in summer?

In most homes, rooms under the attic feel noticeably more comfortable, especially in the afternoon. It won't make up for an undersized or failing AC, leaky ducts or west-facing windows, but it takes a real share of the heat load off.

Can you check my ducts while you're up there?

Yes, and we will. If we see crushed, disconnected or leaky ducts, we'll show you photos and explain your options. It's easiest to fix duct problems while the attic is already open for insulation work.
